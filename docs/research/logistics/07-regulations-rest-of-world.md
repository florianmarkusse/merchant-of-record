# 07: Destination rules for markets outside APAC (US, CA, EU, UK, CH, NO, GCC, LatAm, Africa)

Researched 06-Nov-2026. Scenario: Vietnam-origin apparel and fashion accessories,
sold B2C by us as the foreign merchant of record, shipped DDP (delivered duty
paid) by express courier. The entity is a Singapore company or a Dutch BV.

Legend: **[P]** = a primary or official source was read. **[S]** = secondary
sources only (advisers, carriers, trade press). **UNVERIFIED** = not
confirmed, or sources conflict. HS 61/62 = garments, 64 = footwear, 42 =
bags and leather goods. LVG = low-value goods.

The big picture for 2025–2026: low-value duty exemptions are going away
everywhere. The US ended de minimis (2025, made indefinite 2026). The EU put
a flat €3 duty on each item from 01-Jul-2026 and a €2 handling fee from
01-Nov-2026, with full duty from 01-Jul-2028. The UK ends its £135 duty relief
by October 2028 at the latest. Brazil went the other way (0% federal tax up
to US$50 since 12-May-2026). Chile now taxes items under US$500 at checkout.
In every market the liability moves to the seller or platform, which means
to us as MoR.

## 1. United States

### 1.1 De minimis and valuation
- **Section 321 ($800 de minimis) is suspended for all countries and all
  transport modes.** EO 14324 (July 2025) suspended it worldwide from
  29-Aug-2025. After the Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling of 20-Feb-2026, EO 14388
  continued the suspension, and CBP confirmed it on 23-Feb-2026 [S: GHY].
- CBP then published two interim final rules on 24-Jun-2026 that make the
  suspension **indefinite** under 19 U.S.C. 1321(a)(2)(C), so it no longer
  depends on IEEPA [P: FR 2026-12669, CBP Dec. 26-13, 91 FR 37801; companion
  rule FR 2026-12670 for non-mail]:
  - non-mail: effective 24-Jun-2026;
  - mail: de minimis suspension effective 24-Jun-2026, new postal informal
    entry process effective 24-Jul-2026.
- Statute: the 2025 reconciliation act ("One Big Beautiful Bill") repeals
  commercial de minimis by law from 01-Jul-2027 [S, recalled, UNVERIFIED
  wording]. In practice there is no way back to $800 for commercial
  parcels.
- Litigation over the suspension continues at the CIT and the Federal
  Circuit. Sources conflict on where it stands: one says the CIT upheld the
  suspension (13-Aug-2026), another says the case is stayed. UNVERIFIED, and
  no change is expected in practice.
- Valuation: transaction value (FOB basis, so international freight is
  excluded). Only gifts and accompanied personal baggage are still exempt.

### 1.2 Tariffs on Vietnam-origin goods (as of 2026-11)
- **IEEPA "reciprocal" 20% (and the 40% transshipment penalty): dead.**
  SCOTUS held on 20-Feb-2026 that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs.
  Collection stopped for entries from 24-Feb-2026. Refunds of past IEEPA
  duties go through the CIT, with no automatic process [S: GHY, AirLift].
- **Section 122 surcharge of 10%** (HTS 9903.03.01) ran 24-Feb-2026 to
  24-Jul-2026 and has **expired** [S: GHY].
- **Section 301 of 12.5% on "products of Vietnam"** applies from
  24-Jul-2026, HTS 9903.05.84, USTR notice 91 FR 47318 (published
  28-Jul-2026). The finding: Vietnam does not impose and enforce a ban on
  imports made with forced labour. The same action covers about 60
  economies. There is **no low-value exemption**. It stacks on the MFN rate
  [S: AirLift tracker, Vietnam Briefing; FR notice not read directly].
  Status: **in force, high confidence, primary text UNVERIFIED by us.**
- Pending risk:
  - Section 301 IP investigation against Vietnam (opened 29-May-2026/06-03,
    **final determination due 29-Nov-2026**);
  - excess-capacity investigation (opened 11-Mar-2026).
  - Either could add more duty. The US–Vietnam deal announced 26-Oct-2025
    has no signed text with a rate [S: STR, AirLift].
- MFN apparel rates (HTSUS column 1) are high and vary by fibre:
  - cotton knits often 16–20%;
  - synthetic knits and woven up to 28–32%;
  - footwear from 8.5% to 37.5% or more;
  - handbags (4202) up to about 20%.
  - Typical landed duty on a polyester top: about 32% + 12.5% = **about
    44%** [S, typical HTS values; check per line].
- Origin and transshipment: the specific IEEPA transshipment penalty fell
  with IEEPA. Origin still follows substantial transformation (textile rules
  in 19 CFR 102.21: the country where the garment is assembled, or where
  knit-to-shape parts are knitted). CBP enforcement on Vietnam as a hub for
  Chinese fabric or goods remains intense (EAPA, UFLPA detentions). Keep
  origin evidence per SKU, because the 12.5% depends on "product of
  Vietnam".

### 1.3 Entry types, bond, importer of record
- Non-mail (express): **informal entry (Type 11) up to $2,500, formal
  (Type 01) above.** De minimis Type 86 and Section 321 data-pipeline entries
  are gone.
  - Express carriers file consolidated informal entries for DDP shipments
    using their broker licence.
  - Goods with Chapter 99 duties (Section 301) can still clear informally
    by express [S].
  - MPF applies, and MPF on informal entries is small (a few USD, UNVERIFIED
    exact 2026 figure).
- Mail: **new postal informal entry process** for shipments up to $2,500
  [P: FR 2026-12669]:
  - the filer must have the right to make entry (owner or purchaser, or a
    licensed broker), so not the postal operator;
  - the filer needs a **basic importation bond** (single or continuous);
  - submission is a spreadsheet with 10-digit HTS, origin, value and
    tracking, with duty paid via Pay.gov by the 7th of the following month;
  - an Entry Type 13 electronic mail test started 22-Sep-2026.
  - Goods subject to **Chapter 99 duties (this includes VN Section 301)** or
    to an FTA claim must use **formal entry**. The compliance date was
    22-Oct-2026 and was **postponed to 20-Jan-2027** [S: Thomson Reuters,
    22-Oct-2026 final rule]. So from 20-Jan-2027, VN-origin goods sent by post
    need a formal entry each, which makes **postal impractical for VN
    goods**. Use express.
- Foreign seller as IOR: a **non-resident corporation may be importer of
  record** (19 CFR 141.18) [recalled, standard CBP practice]. It needs:
  - CBP Form 5106 for an importer number;
  - a resident agent for service of process;
  - a continuous bond (minimum $50,000), with a surety that often wants
    collateral from foreign entities.
  - In practice, at low volume, the express carrier's DDP product ("bill
    duties and taxes to shipper") is used: the carrier's broker files the
    informal entry and bills us. We stay seller and payer, but not
    necessarily IOR of record. **Who is IOR in carrier DDP needs
    confirmation with each carrier (UNVERIFIED).**
- No federal sales tax at import. State sales tax: marketplace and remote
  seller economic nexus (typically $100k or 200 transactions per state)
  applies to us as a remote seller. That is a separate compliance stream,
  outside customs.

### 1.4 Documents and product compliance
- Commercial invoice with:
  - an accurate description;
  - 10-digit HTS (needed for Type 11 and postal);
  - country of origin;
  - manufacturer ID (MID) for formal entries;
  - fibre content.
- Textile Fiber Products Identification Act (FTC):
  - fibre content label, country of origin, and RN number or company name;
  - care labelling rule;
  - "Made in Vietnam" marking (19 USC 1304).
- CPSC rules: children's apparel needs a Children's Product Certificate and
  lead/phthalate testing; drawstrings; flammability (16 CFR 1610).
- California Prop 65 warnings where relevant.
- UFLPA: no Xinjiang cotton. Keep traceability, because CBP detains
  apparel for this.

### 1.5 DDP in practice
- DHL Express, FedEx and UPS all sell DDP with duties billed to the shipper
  account. The carrier adds brokerage and disbursement fees (often $10–20+
  per entry, UNVERIFIED current).
- The landed-cost quote must include MFN + 12.5% Section 301 + MPF + carrier
  fees, and must be re-checked after 29-Nov-2026.

### 1.6 Returns
- Unused-merchandise / rejected-merchandise drawback (19 USC 1313(c)):
  **99% of duties back** when goods are returned (exported) within 5 years.
  This takes a drawback filing via a broker, so it is only economic when
  batched.
- Section 301 duties are generally drawback-eligible. The IEEPA duties were
  not [S, recalled, UNVERIFIED for 9903.05.84].
- Practical alternative: liquidate returns domestically (US returns hub) and
  do not re-export.

**Rating: HARD.** About 30–45% duty on synthetic apparel, policy
volatility (a third tariff regime in 2026, another Section 301 determination
on 29-Nov-2026), and the postal channel closing to VN goods from 2027-01.
It is still workable via express DDP. SG vs NL: **no difference.**

## 2. Canada

### 2.1 De minimis and valuation
- For goods **not** of US or Mexico origin (so Vietnam): **CAD 20
  duty-and-tax remission threshold** for both courier and post. The
  CAD 150 duty / CAD 40 tax CUSMA thresholds apply **only to US or Mexico
  origin goods**, so they are irrelevant for us [P: CBSA Customs Notice
  20-18]. No 2026 change was found.
- Courier Low Value Shipment (CLVS) programme: courier clearance up to
  CAD 3,300 [P: CBSA].
- Valuation: transaction value, FOB at the place of direct shipment to
  Canada. GST/HST is charged on the duty-paid value.

### 2.2 Foreign seller registration
- GST 5%, plus HST/PST by province (HST up to 15%).
- The simplified GST/HST registration (from 01-Jul-2021) covers digital
  services and goods supplied **from Canadian warehouses** or via
  platforms. Goods **shipped directly from abroad** have their GST/HST
  collected at the border.
- A non-resident can register normally (voluntarily) to recover input
  taxes as a Non-Resident Importer (NRI).
- CARM (since 21-Oct-2024): NRIs need a Business Number, a CARM Client
  Portal account and **their own financial security (bond)** for Release
  Prior to Payment. Otherwise the courier or broker acts as importer on its
  security [S].
- For low volume, carrier DDP with the courier as importer is simplest.

### 2.3 Duty and FTA
- MFN apparel: 17–18% (ch. 61/62); footwear up to 18–20%; bags about 8–11%
  [S, typical].
- **CPTPP applies (Vietnam and Canada are both parties)**:
  - apparel duty is largely 0 for originating goods by 2026 (phase-outs
    from 2019) [S, UNVERIFIED per line];
  - the rule of origin is **yarn-forward**, with a short-supply list.
    Garments sewn in VN from Chinese fabric generally do not qualify.
  - Certification: an origin certification by exporter or importer. For
    low-value goods (≤ CAD 1,600 per CPTPP practice) a statement may
    suffice [UNVERIFIED].
  - In practice, couriers rarely claim CPTPP on CLVS shipments unless the
    shipper provides origin data.
- Vietnam's GPT status: UNVERIFIED. GPT largely excludes apparel anyway.

### 2.4 Documents and compliance
- Textile Labelling Act: **bilingual (EN/FR) fibre content** plus dealer
  identity (CA identification number or name and address).
- Care labelling is voluntary.
- Canada Consumer Product Safety Act; children's sleepwear and drawstring
  rules.

### 2.5 DDP in practice
- Express DDP with the courier billing the shipper is standard, and a CAD 20
  threshold means almost every order pays.
- Canada Post plus a broker is cheaper but slower. There was labour
  disruption at Canada Post in 2024–2025.

### 2.6 Returns
- Refund of duty and GST for goods returned to the vendor: Customs Act
  s.74/76 refund claims (B2 adjustment). Time limits are 1 year for some
  grounds and up to 4 years for others [S, UNVERIFIED specifics].
  Brokers batch these.

**Rating: MEDIUM.** Predictable rules. Duty is high unless CPTPP origin can
be proven, which is hard because of yarn-forward. Bilingual labels are
needed. SG vs NL: **no difference.**

## 3. European Union

### 3.1 De minimis and valuation
- **VAT:** no de minimis since 01-Jul-2021. IOSS covers consignments of
  **intrinsic value ≤ €150**: VAT at the destination Member State rate is
  charged at checkout, and the parcel is released on the IOSS number.
- **Customs duty:** the €150 duty exemption **ended on 30-Jun-2026**.
  - Council Regulation (EU) 2026/382, with Delegated Regulation 2026/1022
    and Implementing Regulation 2026/1200 [P: TAXUD].
  - From **01-Jul-2026 until 01-Jul-2028**, a **temporary flat €3 customs duty
    per item** applies to distance-sale consignments ≤ €150. It applies
    under any VAT scheme (IOSS, special arrangements or standard), not only
    IOSS.
  - "Item" means **tariff classification, not quantity**: five T-shirts =
    €3, one T-shirt + one watch = €6 [P: TAXUD guidance, 08-Jun-2026;
    Commission news 29-Jun-2026].
  - The declarant pays: the seller, the IOSS holder or the indirect
    representative.
  - **Preferential origin exception:** goods that benefit from a
    preferential agreement are excluded from the €3 **only if VAT was not
    collected through IOSS and the goods are declared on a full H1
    declaration** [P: TAXUD]. So **EVFTA cannot reduce the €3 on IOSS
    parcels.**
- **Union handling fee: €2** from **01-Nov-2026**, set by Delegated
  Regulation (EU) 2026/2152 and Implementing Regulation 2026/2403 for
  guarantees and data [P: TAXUD customs reform page].
  - It applies per item like the €3, regardless of value, so consignments
    above €150 sold in distance sales pay it too, and it is permanent
    [P: TAXUD guidance of 5-Oct-2026, §3.2].
  - **VAT on the fee** (checked 11-Nov-2026): the €2 is part of the VAT
    taxable amount as an incidental expense (VAT Directive Arts. 73,
    78(b), 86(1)(b)). Under the special arrangements and the standard
    procedure, import VAT is charged on it; under IOSS there is no import
    VAT, but if the fee is charged to the buyer it goes into the IOSS
    return [P: TAXUD guidance "The temporary EUR 3 customs duty and the
    Union handling fee", version of 5-Oct-2026, §9.2].
  - **VAT on the €3 duty:** the guidance (§9.1) leaves the VAT schemes
    unchanged. Without IOSS, import VAT is charged on customs value plus
    duties due by reason of importation (VAT Directive Art. 86(1)(a)),
    so the €3 is in the base. Under IOSS, VAT is collected at sale on the
    price and the €3 is not added [P: Art. 86(1)(a); S: Studio Savia,
    citing an Italian customs notice of 9-Sep-2026].
    https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs/eu-customs-reform_en ;
    https://studiosavia.com/en/eu-3-euro-customs-duty-low-value-parcels/
  - Reportedly **not refunded on returns** [S].
  - Italy's own €2 national fee was postponed to 01-Jan-2027, with repeal
    pending [S, UNVERIFIED].
- **From 01-Jul-2028:** normal duty rates on all consignments, and the EU
  Customs Data Hub becomes mandatory for e-commerce [P: TAXUD].
- **EU customs reform** (adopted 16-Sep-2026, in force 21-Sep-2026) [P:
  TAXUD]:
  - platforms and sellers become the "importer for distance sales" and are
    liable for duty, and must ensure product compliance through an EU
    responsible operator;
  - non-EU sellers must report each sale to the Data Hub;
  - the EU Customs Authority (Lille) starts in 2027.
- Valuation:
  - duty on CIF value at the EU border;
  - import VAT on customs value + duty + transport to destination;
  - the IOSS €150 test uses intrinsic value, excluding shipping.

### 3.2 Foreign seller registration and intermediary
- **SG entity:** a non-EU seller **must appoint an EU-established IOSS
  intermediary**. The exception is sellers established in a country with a
  VAT mutual-assistance agreement with the EU: Norway is listed, Singapore
  is not. The intermediary is jointly liable, typically costs a few thousand
  EUR per year, and often requires a bank guarantee [recalled from TAXUD
  IOSS guidance, high confidence].
- **NL BV:** registers for IOSS **directly in NL**, with no intermediary.
  It can also:
  - import above €150 itself, using the NL **Article 23 import-VAT
    deferment licence** (cash-flow neutral);
  - or import in bulk into an EU warehouse and sell B2C from it under
    **Union OSS**, a distance-sales-within-EU model that is impossible for
    an SG entity without NL/EU VAT registration and a fiscal
    representative.
  - The BV can be the **EU responsible person** for GPSR and the EU
    "importer for distance sales" under the new customs code.
- Above €150 (no IOSS): import VAT and duty are paid at the border. A
  non-EU seller needs an EU EORI plus an **indirect customs
  representative** (the courier). An NL BV can be the declarant/importer
  itself.

### 3.3 Duty and EVFTA
- EU MFN rates:
  - ch. 61/62 apparel: **12%**;
  - footwear (64): 8% (leather uppers) to 16.9–17% (rubber, plastic or
    textile uppers);
  - handbags (4202): about 3% (leather) to 9.7% (plastic or textile);
  - imitation jewellery (7117): 4%.
- **EVFTA (since 01-Aug-2020):** EU duties on VN apparel are phased out over
  up to 7 years, so most lines are at 0% or close to it in 2026, and the
  phase-out is complete by about 2027 [S, UNVERIFIED per line].
  - Rule of origin for garments: **double transformation (fabric-forward)**.
    The fabric must originate in VN or the EU. Korean fabric is allowed via
    cumulation. Chinese fabric does **not** qualify.
  - Proof of origin: a **statement on origin** on the invoice. Any exporter
    may make one for consignments ≤ €6,000; above that, a registered
    exporter (REX) or an EUR.1 from MOIT.
  - **Practicality for parcels:**
    - until 01-Jul-2028 it is irrelevant for IOSS parcels (flat €3 either
      way);
    - above €150 it is useful: 12% becomes 0% if the fabric is VN or EU
      origin;
    - after 01-Jul-2028 it becomes valuable for all parcels if the origin
      statement is passed in the courier data. Few courier B2C flows
      support preferential claims today (UNVERIFIED by carrier).

### 3.4 Product compliance
- **GPSR (Reg. 2023/988), applies since 13-Dec-2024:** products sold online
  to EU consumers need an **EU-established responsible economic operator**.
  - That operator's name, postal and electronic address must be on the
    product, packaging or accompanying document, and in the online listing.
  - The listing also needs product identification, warnings and safety
    information in the local language.
  - The NL BV can be this operator; an SG entity needs a paid EU
    responsible person.
- **Textile Labelling Regulation (EU) 1007/2011:** fibre composition label
  in the Member State's official language(s).
- Care labels are voluntary (GINETEX) in most Member States, but France
  requires French under the Toubon law.
- REACH restrictions (azo dyes, nickel in accessories, PFAS phase-outs).
- **Textile EPR:**
  - EU-wide: Waste Framework Directive revision 2025/1892, in force
    16-Oct-2025. All Member States must run textile and footwear EPR schemes
    **by 17-Apr-2028**, covering clothing, footwear and accessories [S].
    Distance sellers into a Member State are producers there and generally
    need an **authorised representative** in each Member State (UNVERIFIED
    exact transposition).
  - **France:** textile EPR since 2007 (Refashion), with eco-modulated
    fees. Foreign distance sellers must register and get a unique
    identifier (IDU). Marketplaces must verify compliance.
  - **NL:** UPV Textiel since 01-Jul-2023 (Stichting UPV Textiel). It covers
    consumer clothing and household textiles, and foreign sellers need an
    authorised representative.
  - Also check Hungary (2023), Spain, Italy and others transposing.
- Packaging EPR (Germany VerpackG / LUCID registration for anyone shipping
  packaged goods to German consumers, French Triman, etc.) applies too, and
  the PPWR applies from 12-Aug-2026.

### 3.5 DDP in practice
- **≤ €150:**
  - the courier lodges an H7 (super-reduced dataset) with the **IOSS
    number**;
  - from 01-Jul-2026 it adds the €3/item duty and from 01-Nov-2026 the €2
    handling fee, both billed to the shipper's DDP account;
  - consumer VAT is already collected by us at checkout and declared in the
    monthly IOSS return.
- **> €150:** a full H1 declaration. The courier acts as indirect
  representative and pays duty and import VAT, then bills us. The buyer
  pays nothing at the door.
- Typical NL/BE hubs: DHL (BRU/LEJ), UPS (CGN), FedEx (CDG/LGG).

### 3.6 Returns
- **IOSS VAT:** correct the VAT in the IOSS return (negative adjustment)
  for refunded sales, within 3 years.
- **Customs duty:** repayment under UCC Art. 118 (goods rejected as
  defective or not in conformity with the contract) or Art. 119/121.
  Consumer-withdrawal returns are treated inconsistently between Member
  States.
- Whether the €3 and €2 are refundable: €2 reportedly no; €3 UNVERIFIED.
- Import VAT paid above €150 can be refunded via re-export claims or
  through credit notes for an NL BV with Art. 23 deferment.

**Rating: MEDIUM.** The rules are clear and DDP-friendly. Until 2028 the
cost is predictable (VAT + €3/item + €2 + IOSS intermediary). The 2028
customs reform and textile EPR rollouts add heavy compliance (GPSR
responsible person, EPR per country). **NL BV helps a lot:** no IOSS
intermediary, it is itself the GPSR responsible operator and EU importer,
it can use Art. 23 deferment and OSS, and it can become the "importer for
distance sales". With an SG entity, budget for an IOSS intermediary, a GPSR
responsible person and EPR representatives.

## 4. United Kingdom

### 4.1 De minimis and valuation
- **VAT:** no LVCR since 01-Jan-2021. For consignments of **≤ £135** (intrinsic
  value), the **seller charges UK VAT (20%) at the point of sale** and the
  goods enter without import VAT. Above £135, import VAT is paid at the
  border (postponed VAT accounting is available to UK VAT-registered
  importers).
- **Customs duty:** currently **relieved for consignments ≤ £135**, except
  excise goods. **The relief is being removed**:
  - announced at Budget 26-Nov-2025;
  - consultation 26-Nov-2025 to 06-Mar-2026, response published
    **13-Jul-2026**;
  - at Tax Update Day 2026 the deadline was accelerated from March 2029 to
    **October 2028 at the latest** (the appointed day is set by Treasury
    regulations), with legislation in the Finance Bill 2026-27
    [P: GOV.UK policy paper and consultation response].
  - Under the new regime, sellers and marketplaces pay customs duty on LVIs
    (reported as **quarterly** payments [S]).
  - An overseas seller may appoint a **UK fiscal representative** who is
    jointly and severally liable. The paper costs this as an ongoing
    expense, which suggests it will be required for overseas sellers
    (UNVERIFIED as mandatory) [P].
  - Excluded: excise goods, trade-defence goods, restricted goods and
    non-ad-valorem tariffs. The £39 gift relief is kept.
- Valuation: duty on CIF value at the UK border.

### 4.2 Foreign seller registration
- A non-established taxable person selling ≤ £135 goods to UK consumers
  must **register for UK VAT from the first sale (no threshold)**. A
  fiscal representative is not normally required for VAT (HMRC may ask for
  security) [recalled, HMRC VAT and overseas goods sold to customers in the
  UK, high confidence].
- A GB EORI is needed for consignments above £135 if we act as importer.
- **Both SG and NL are "overseas" for the UK after Brexit.** The NL BV has
  no advantage, apart from EU–UK TCA origin, which is irrelevant for VN
  goods.

### 4.3 Duty and UKVFTA
- UK Global Tariff: apparel **12%**, footwear up to 16–17%, bags 2–9.7%
  (mirrors the EU).
- **UKVFTA (since 01-Jan-2021, mirrors EVFTA):** phasing to 0 on the same
  schedule. Same double-transformation fabric rule; the origin statement is
  made by the exporter (REX-based or ≤ £5,500 equivalent, UNVERIFIED
  threshold).
- Until the relief ends (≤ Oct 2028) duty is 0 on ≤ £135 anyway. Above
  £135 the FTA saves 12% when the fabric qualifies.
- CPTPP: the UK joined CPTPP on 15-Dec-2024 and Vietnam is a party. This is
  an alternative preferential route with fabric-forward rules for garments
  (UNVERIFIED which route is better).

### 4.4 Compliance
- UK textile labelling (retained 1007/2011): English fibre composition.
- General Product Safety Regulations 2005; the Product Regulation and
  Metrology Act 2025 enables new online-marketplace and seller duties.
  **No UK equivalent of the GPSR responsible person yet (UNVERIFIED for
  2026 secondary legislation).**
- UK packaging EPR (from 2025) applies to UK-established producers. A
  non-UK seller's obligation falls on the UK importer or marketplace
  [S, UNVERIFIED].
- Textile EPR consultation: not yet in force.

### 4.5 DDP in practice
- ≤ £135: our UK VAT number goes on the commercial invoice. The courier
  clears without collecting VAT and with no duty today.
- > £135: the courier pays duty and import VAT and bills us DDP. Import VAT
  is recoverable on our UK VAT return if we are the importer (with an EORI
  and PVA).

### 4.6 Returns
- UK VAT: issue a credit note and adjust the VAT return.
- Duty and import VAT on rejected imports: claim on form C285 (Rejected
  Imports Relief, within a time limit, goods exported or destroyed). The
  Returned Goods Relief is for UK exports coming back, not this case.

**Rating: EASY (now) → MEDIUM (from Oct 2028).** Today: one UK VAT
registration, no duty ≤ £135, English labels. After 2028: duty on every
parcel and likely a fiscal representative. SG vs NL: **no difference.**

## 5. Switzerland

### 5.1 De minimis and valuation
- **Customs duty: 0% on industrial products, including all apparel,
  footwear and bags, since 01-Jan-2024** (abolition of industrial tariffs)
  [recalled, SECO; high confidence]. A statistical fee and the
  import-VAT basis still apply.
- Import VAT 8.1% (standard rate since 2024). It is **waived when the VAT
  amount is ≤ CHF 5**, i.e. goods worth up to about CHF 62 [S].
- Mail-order rule (since 01-Jan-2019): a foreign seller whose **small
  consignments** into CH reach **CHF 100,000 per year** must register for
  Swiss VAT. It then charges Swiss VAT on **all** its sales to CH and is
  deemed the supplier/importer [S, consistent across advisers]. Platform
  rules followed from 01-Jan-2025.
- Valuation for VAT: price plus transport to the destination.

### 5.2 Registration
- A foreign VAT-registered business needs a **Swiss tax representative**
  (domiciled agent) [S]. EU OSS/IOSS does not apply.
- Below CHF 100k of small-consignment sales, the courier collects import
  VAT DDP. Swiss couriers charge noticeable clearance fees (often CHF 10–20+
  on DDP, UNVERIFIED).

### 5.3 Compliance and returns
- Swiss product safety law (PrSG/PrSV) is broadly EU-aligned.
- Swiss fibre labelling is not mandatory federally; consumer information
  rules apply [UNVERIFIED].
- Returns: import VAT can be refunded on re-export via the courier (Passar
  correction or form request), and couriers often charge for it.

**Rating: EASY.** No duty on apparel. VAT can be paid DDP by the courier
until CHF 100k. SG vs NL: **no difference** (the NL BV is not EU-advantaged
in CH).

## 6. Norway (VOEC)

- **VOEC (since 01-Apr-2020):** foreign sellers and marketplaces of LVG
  register in the VOEC register once sales to Norway exceed **NOK 50,000
  over 12 months**; voluntary registration from the first sale is allowed
  [P: Skatteetaten VOEC register page, checked 10-Nov-2026].
  - Items under **NOK 3,000 per item** carry **25% Norwegian VAT at the
    point of sale**.
  - VAT is filed and paid **quarterly**.
  - The **VOEC number must be stated digitally on every shipment**
    [P: Skatteetaten VOEC page].
  - **No representative is required.**
- Customs duty: VOEC goods under NOK 3,000 per item are generally **free of
  customs duty**. Above that, apparel is dutiable (Norway keeps duties on
  some textiles, roughly up to 10.7%, UNVERIFIED) and import VAT is paid at
  the border.
- Changes from **01-Jul-2026** affecting postal collection were reported.
  Checked 10-Nov-2026: the 01-Jul-2026 change found on toll.no is the EU's
  €3 duty on parcels entering the EU, which affects Norwegian exports, not
  imports into Norway. No change to Norway's own NOK 3,000 VOEC limit or
  postal collection was found [P: toll.no; regjeringen.no Finance Ministry
  changes from 01-Jul-2026]. A consumer site reports Posten's clearance fee
  is waived for VOEC parcels under NOK 3,000 [S: bytt.no].
- Returns: VAT credit in the VOEC return for refunded sales.
- Compliance: EEA, so GPSR applies via the EEA Agreement (UNVERIFIED
  incorporation status). Norwegian-language labelling for consumer
  information.

**Rating: EASY.** One light registration, no rep, mostly duty-free under
NOK 3,000. SG vs NL: **no difference** for VOEC. Norway has an EU VAT
mutual-assistance agreement, which is irrelevant here.

## 7. GCC: UAE and Saudi Arabia

- **UAE:**
  - GCC common customs duty is 5% on CIF. Courier consignments ≤ **AED
    1,000** are duty-exempt (except excise and restricted goods) [S].
  - VAT 5%: sources do not confirm a VAT de minimis, and VAT is generally
    collected on imports (UNVERIFIED).
  - Dubai Customs exempts e-commerce shipments ≤ AED 1,000 from its fees
    from 03-Aug-2026, with a 60-day returns exemption [S: Al Khaleej].
  - There is no simplified VAT regime for non-resident goods sellers. The
    courier collects at import, and a non-resident must register for VAT
    only if it makes taxable supplies in the UAE (e.g. as importer of record
    with local sales), with a tax agent [recalled, UNVERIFIED].
- **Saudi Arabia:**
  - customs duty de minimis **SAR 1,000** for courier and personal
    consignments, with **VAT 15% on all imports** [S];
  - a flat SAR 15 customs fee on e-commerce shipments ≤ SAR 1,000 (2024
    rule) [S: EY];
  - a non-resident supplier of goods that imports in its own name must
    register for VAT (no threshold for non-residents) and needs a tax
    representative (recalled, UNVERIFIED).
  - SFDA/SASO product rules: apparel generally needs SABER conformity for
    commercial imports; B2C courier is usually exempt below thresholds
    (UNVERIFIED).
- Apparel duty is 5% (some GCC lines 5–12%). No FTA with Vietnam.
- Arabic labelling is required for retail goods in KSA (UNVERIFIED for B2C
  parcels).
- DDP: DHL and Aramex DDP are common. Courier clearance fees are small.
- Returns: duty refund on re-export within the GCC rules (typically within
  a set period, via the courier), VAT credit (UNVERIFIED).

**Rating: MEDIUM.** Low duty (5%) and courier DDP work fine, but VAT
registration and representative questions for a non-resident are unclear.
SG vs NL: **no difference.**

## 8. Latin America

### 8.1 Brazil (Remessa Conforme)
- **Remessa Conforme (RC, since 2023-08):** a Receita Federal certification
  for e-commerce companies (platforms and foreign retailers). Tax is
  collected at checkout, declared in advance (DIR), with priority release.
  - Up to US$50 (customs value incl. freight and insurance), buyer is an
    individual (CPF):
    - **0% federal import tax since 12-May-2026** (Portaria MF 1.342/2026,
      under MP 1.357/2026);
    - the MP was **converted into law: approved by Congress on 03-Sep-2026,
      sanctioned on 10-Sep-2026** [S, several Brazilian outlets];
    - **before that** the rate was 20% from 01-Aug-2024 to 11-May-2026.
  - US$50.01–3,000: **60% with a US$30 deduction** (raised from US$20 by
    Portaria MF 1.342/2026) [S: DHL Brazil, Senado, Econet; checked
    10-Nov-2026]. DHL says the act for a 30% rate has not been published;
    one column claims 30% for RC companies. Treat 60% − US$30 as current.
  - **ICMS (state): 17–20%** depending on the state, charged "por dentro"
    (grossed up). Ten states raised it to 20% in April 2025.
  - A purchase in the name of a company (CNPJ) falls under the general
    regime (60% + ICMS) [S: DHL].
  - Outside RC: 60% import tax + ICMS, with slower clearance.
- Registration: a foreign seller can be certified. Integration with
  Receita's systems and a local legal representative/partner are typically
  required (UNVERIFIED). Most foreign sellers go through a platform or
  logistics partner who is RC-certified.
- Compliance: Portuguese labelling (INMETRO textile labelling rules:
  composition, CNPJ/identification, care symbols) [UNVERIFIED for B2C
  imports].
- Returns: refund of RC tax for returned goods is cumbersome (UNVERIFIED).
  Most sellers treat Brazil as no-returns-in-practice.
- **Rating: MEDIUM-HARD.** Very cheap under US$50 right now, but it needs
  RC certification (a partner), the rules change politically, there are
  steep cliffs above US$50, and returns are painful. SG vs NL: no
  difference.

### 8.2 Mexico
- Courier simplified regime ("tasa global", Rule 3.7.35 RGCE):
  - non-USMCA goods up to **US$2,500** via registered courier went from
    19% (Jan 2025) to **33.5% flat** from **15-Aug-2025**
    [S: Baker McKenzie, VATupdate];
  - USMCA goods > US$117 pay 19%.
- A December 2025 decree raised MFN duties on 1,463 tariff lines (textiles
  and apparel up to 35–50%) for **non-FTA** countries from 2026 [S].
  **Vietnam has CPTPP with Mexico**, so VN-originating goods with proof of
  origin may avoid the hikes. Whether a **courier shipment of VN goods**
  can claim CPTPP instead of the 33.5% flat rate is **UNVERIFIED**
  (probably only via a formal entry with a customs broker and a CPTPP
  origin certification).
- VAT 16% is included in or collected alongside the courier flat rate.
- Mexico requires foreign digital-services providers to register. Foreign
  sellers of goods via platforms have platform withholding. Direct foreign
  e-shops are not registered (UNVERIFIED).
- NOM-004 textile labelling (Spanish, fibre content, importer RFC) is
  required for commercial imports.
- **Rating: HARD.** A 33.5% flat rate on courier imports, plus labelling
  that needs a Mexican importer RFC for NOM compliance.

### 8.3 Chile
- Since **25-Oct-2025** (Ley 21.713, Ley de Cumplimiento Tributario):
  - foreign sellers and platforms charge **19% IVA at checkout** on sales
    to individuals of goods ≤ **US$500** (including shipping);
  - registration is in the **SII simplified regime**, open since
    01-Aug-2025: no Chilean domicile required, monthly or quarterly filing;
  - **customs duty is exempt** for these consignments ≤ US$500 [S: SII
    news 22-Sep-2025, FedEx, Carey].
- Above US$500: IVA + 6% general duty. Chile has an FTA with Vietnam
  (VCFTA) and CPTPP, which help formal imports.
- **Rating: EASY-MEDIUM.** A clear registration model, no rep, no duty up to
  US$500. SG vs NL: no difference.

### 8.4 Colombia
- The long-standing courier/postal regime: ≤ US$200, duty-exempt with a
  single 10% rate, IVA 19%.
  - Decreto Legislativo 1474/2025 cut the IVA-free cap to **US$50** for
    2026, but the Constitutional Court suspended it provisionally on
    29-Jan-2026 (Auto 082/2026). Since then DIAN applies the **US$200 IVA
    exclusion again** (Oficio DIAN 1470/2026). The decree never touched
    the duty: the 10% single rate up to US$200 FOB stands (Oficio DIAN
    407/2026) [P: DIAN normograma, checked 10-Nov-2026]. A final Court
    ruling could change this again; whether the US$200 is FOB or customs
    value is still being asked of DIAN.
- There is no specific foreign seller VAT registration for goods. The
  courier collects.
- **Rating: MEDIUM** (frequent changes, but courier DDP works).

## 9. Africa (brief)

- **South Africa:**
  - **the low-value concession changed on 01-Sep-2024**: shipments ≤ R500
    were previously 20% flat with no VAT, and now carry **15% VAT plus the
    20% flat rate**;
  - above R500 the normal tariff applies, with **clothing typically at 40–45%
    duty** + VAT [S, rate per line UNVERIFIED].
  - No foreign-seller VAT scheme for goods.
  - **Rating: HARD** (protective apparel tariffs).
- **Nigeria:**
  - VAT 7.5%; non-resident suppliers must register and charge VAT under
    the Finance Acts and the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (effective 2026)
    [UNVERIFIED];
  - **finished textiles and garments have been on Nigeria's import
    prohibition list** (Schedule 3: "all types of foreign textiles and
    garments"), with only limited exceptions [recalled, UNVERIFIED current
    list];
  - FX and payment friction.
  - **Rating: HARD / likely not viable** for apparel.

### Where the entity choice matters
- **EU is the only market where the entity choice changes much.** The NL BV:
  - avoids the mandatory IOSS intermediary, with its cost and bank
    guarantee;
  - is itself the GPSR responsible economic operator and the future
    "importer for distance sales";
  - can import in bulk under Art. 23 deferment and sell B2C via Union OSS
    from an EU stock point;
  - can act as authorised representative/producer for textile EPR in NL
    (other Member States may still need local registration).
- An SG entity needs about three EU service providers: an IOSS
  intermediary, a GPSR responsible person and EPR representatives.
- UK, CH, NO, US, CA and the rest: both entities count as foreign, so no
  difference. Norway's EU mutual-assistance agreement does not benefit
  either entity.

### Suggested launch order (from a rules point of view)
1. UK (until 2028), Switzerland, Norway, Chile: simple and low duty.
2. EU via an NL BV (or SG + intermediary), budgeting €3 + €2 per item until
   2028.
3. Canada: medium.
4. US: hard but large. Express DDP only, re-quote after 29-Nov-2026.
5. Brazil via an RC partner.
6. Mexico and South Africa only with a local partner. Skip Nigeria.
