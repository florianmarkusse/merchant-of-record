## 1. Australia (deep)

### 1.1 Jurisdictional reach: why we are the "supplier"
- The ACL (Competition and Consumer Act 2010, Sch 2) applies to conduct outside Australia
  by bodies corporate that "carry on business within Australia" (CCA s5). *ACCC v Valve*
  [2016] FCA 196 held that a foreign online seller with no Australian premises carried on
  business in Australia by selling to Australian consumers. A Singapore or Dutch MoR
  selling to Australian buyers is therefore treated as an ACL supplier. [A, prior
  knowledge, case well known; **UNVERIFIED** in this session]
- The ACCC says online businesses "at every stage of the supply chain must follow Australian
  product safety laws" [P: productsafety.gov.au/product-safety-laws/compliance/selling-online].
- As MoR we are the seller of record, so **every ACL obligation below lands on us**. The VN
  merchant is not an ACL supplier in AU, so its duties to us are contractual (labels, test
  reports, warranties, indemnity).
- Penalties for breaching safety or information standards (ss106, 136): for corporations,
  the greater of A$50m, 3x the benefit, or 30% of adjusted turnover (raised Nov 2022). [prior
  knowledge, **UNVERIFIED** here]

### 1.2 Consumer Goods (Care Labelling) Information Standard 2023 [P]
- (a) **Requirements.** A permanent care label, legible, in English. It covers all parts of
  the article (trims, linings, fasteners), uses wording or symbols per AS/NZS 1957 or
  ISO 3758, and warns against treatments that damage the good. If the article cannot be
  washed or dry-cleaned, the label must say so in words. Some items (some gloves, hosiery,
  collars) may carry the information on a removable tag, pamphlet or packaging instead.
- **Covered:** clothing and apparel (including samples and seconds), household textiles,
  furnishings, piece goods and yarns, including leather, suede, skins, furs and
  plastic-coated fabrics.
- **Exempt (relevant to us):** footwear (except hosiery and footwear textile materials),
  hats/headwear, belts, braces and similar accessories, bags and cases, umbrellas,
  handkerchiefs, sporting/gardening/industrial gloves, second-hand goods, single-use articles.
  Scarves, fashion gloves and leather garments **are covered**. Jewellery is not a textile,
  so the standard does not apply.
- (b) Applies to any supply in trade or commerce to AU consumers. There is no carve-out for
  online or cross-border sellers.
- (c) Owed by **us**. The VN manufacturer physically sews the label.
- (d) **Dates:** commenced 5 Sep 2023. Transition 1 ran 5 Sep 2023 to 4 Mar 2024 (old 2010
  notice or new standard); transition 2 ran 5 Mar 2024 to 4 Mar 2025 (ISO 3758:2012 also
  allowed). **From 5 Mar 2025, labels must follow AS/NZS 1957 or ISO 3758 as named in the
  2023 standard.**
- (e) **Enforcement:** the ACCC runs surveillance and test purchases. Small parcels are not
  inspected at the border for care labels; the risk is complaints or ACCC action. Low
  per-parcel risk, but the penalty ceiling is high.
- (f) **Per SKU:** `care_label_present` flag, care instructions text/symbols, label photo,
  and an exemption reason for exempt categories.
- Sources: legislation.gov.au F2023L01187 (instrument + explanatory statement) [P]; SGS,
  TÜV SÜD and Bureau Veritas summaries [A].

### 1.3 Mandatory reporting (ACL s131) and recalls [P]
- (a) Within **2 days** of becoming aware that a death, serious injury or serious illness
  was caused, or may have been caused, by a consumer good we supplied, report to the ACCC
  through the online form. "A supplier must submit a report whether the incident happened in
  Australia or overseas." Online selling is listed as supply. A voluntary recall must be
  notified to the Commonwealth Minister within 2 days (s128).
- (b) Applies to us, including overseas incidents involving goods we also supply in AU.
- (c) **Us.** We can appoint an agent to report on our behalf.
- (d) In force.
- (e) The ACCC has said overseas online sellers often do not know about this duty and has
  signalled enforcement. Treasury is reviewing online marketplace obligations.
- (f) Batch/lot traceability per order line. Build an incident intake → 2-day SLA workflow
  in support tooling.
- Source: productsafety.gov.au "Who must submit a mandatory report" [P].

### 1.4 Mandatory safety standards and bans relevant to the catalogue
- The ACCC lists 3 mandatory standards under "Clothing and fashion accessories" [P]. From
  prior knowledge (**UNVERIFIED** in this session) these are:
  - **Sunglasses and fashion spectacles:** Consumer Goods (Sunglasses and Fashion
    Spectacles) Safety Standard 2017, based on AS/NZS 1067.1:2016. Requirements: lens
    category marking (0–4), a "not suitable for driving" or similar warning for the
    relevant categories, and UV transmittance and robustness requirements. **Relevant: we
    sell sunglasses.** Per SKU: lens category, label artwork, test report (AS/NZS 1067.1
    or equivalent).
  - **Children's nightwear and limited daywear** (AS/NZS 1249): fire-hazard labels and
    fabric/design requirements for sizes 00–14. Kids only.
  - Care labelling (above) may be the third. Or the third may be the ban on
    hazardous drawstrings? (UNVERIFIED; AU has no mandatory drawstring standard to my
    knowledge.)
- Button/coin battery standards: not applicable (no batteries in scope).
- Small high-powered magnets ban (permanent ban, 2012): could catch magnetic jewellery sold
  as separable magnet sets. Check any magnetic-clasp items. **UNVERIFIED** scope.
- **Azo dyes:** no AU ban or mandatory standard on azo dyes in clothing. The ACCC handled
  azo-dye findings in 2014 as voluntary recalls under general ACL powers. **UNVERIFIED**
  that nothing has changed since; worth a supplier declaration aligned to EU REACH Annex
  XVII anyway.
- **Lead/cadmium in jewellery:** no AU mandatory standard for adult jewellery known
  (**UNVERIFIED**). Children's toys have element limits; children's jewellery may count as
  a toy.

### 1.5 General safety provision (GSP)
- **Status Nov 2026: not enacted, and no federal bill found.** A 2026 Chambers practice
  guide and a ~Sep 2026 article in The Conversation both say the ACL still has no general
  safety provision. The Conversation calls for reviving the 2023 Treasury consultation [A/N].
- Unrelated 2026 change: the Competition and Consumer Amendment (Unfair Trading Practices)
  Act passed 2 Jul 2026 and commences 1 Jul 2027. It covers manipulative online practices
  (dark patterns, subscription traps) and affects our checkout UX, not product safety [P/A].
- Design note: if a GSP arrives (modelled on the EU GPSR), it would add a positive duty to
  supply only safe products. Keep test reports and risk assessment per SKU now, so we are
  ready.

### 1.6 PFAS (IChEMS)
- From **1 Jul 2025**, PFOS, PFOA and PFHxS (plus salts, isomers and precursors) are listed
  on IChEMS Schedule 7: import, manufacture, export and use are prohibited. Only narrow
  exemptions apply (therapeutic goods per TGA; trace-contaminant thresholds per secondary
  sources) [P: TGA; A]. States and territories enforce use; import is controlled
  Commonwealth-side.
- Coverage of **imported articles** (for example DWR-coated jackets, bags) is
  **UNVERIFIED**. The DCCEEW page timed out. Secondary sources say products and articles
  are covered, with legacy-in-use exemptions.
- Practical: obtain a supplier declaration "no intentionally added PFAS / no PFOS, PFOA,
  PFHxS" for water-repellent outerwear, bags and footwear. Enforcement on B2C parcels is
  realistically near zero today.

### 1.7 Origin labelling, textile/fur labelling
- AU has **no mandatory fibre-content labelling** (unlike NZ). Country-of-origin labelling
  is mandatory only for food (Country of Origin Food Labelling Information Standard 2016).
  For clothing, origin claims must not be misleading (ACL s18, s29). Commerce (Trade
  Descriptions) rules on imports are narrowed to prescribed goods, and clothing is believed
  no longer prescribed. **UNVERIFIED.**
- **Cat and dog fur:** import of cat or dog fur products has been prohibited under the
  Customs (Prohibited Imports) Regulations since 2004. **UNVERIFIED** current wording. Real
  fur needs a species declaration per SKU; also check CITES for exotic leathers.

### 1.8 Modern Slavery Act 2018 (Cth)
- Reporting entities: consolidated revenue **≥ A$100m** and carrying on business in AU. The
  government kept the threshold after the 2023 review recommended A$50m. On **16 Jul 2026**
  it announced a new "failure to prevent" style criminal offence for entities over A$100m,
  plus non-reporting penalties and due diligence for high-risk sectors. Legislation is
  expected in 2026 but not yet confirmed passed [N: Wolters Kluwer, Baptist World Aid].
- Applies to us only above the threshold (consolidated, possibly counting GMV-based
  revenue as MoR, which matters because MoR revenue is gross). Apparel from VN is a
  high-risk sector. Start collecting factory name, location and audits per merchant.

## 2. New Zealand (deep)

### 2.1 Reach
- The Fair Trading Act 1986 s3 applies to conduct outside NZ by a person resident or
  **carrying on business in NZ**, to the extent it relates to supply of goods in NZ (prior
  knowledge, **UNVERIFIED** wording). A foreign MoR targeting NZ consumers (NZD pricing,
  NZ delivery) is very likely to be found to carry on business in NZ. Assume the rules
  apply to us.
- The Commerce Commission enforces. Pages say obligations attach to "anyone who supplies,
  offers to supply, or advertises the supply of" the goods, "including online auction
  sites" [P]. Penalty for a body corporate breaching a consumer information standard: up to
  NZ$600,000 (FTA s40). **UNVERIFIED** figure.

### 2.2 Consumer Information Standards [P: comcom.govt.nz]
| Standard | Covered | Exempt (relevant) | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Care labelling** (Care Labelling Regulations 2000) | Clothing, household textiles, furnishings, leather, suede, fur, plastic-coated fabrics; made in NZ or overseas | Footwear (except hosiery); haberdashery. Bags, hats and belts are not listed as exempt on the page but are mostly not "textile clothing": **check** | English, legible, permanent, visible on examination, findable before purchase. Symbols may accompany words. Gloves and hosiery may use non-permanent labels. |
| **Fibre content** (Fibre Content Labelling Regulations 2000) | Clothing, socks/hosiery, fabrics | Footwear, belts, corsets, handkerchiefs, **hats**, **bags and cases**, toys | Fibres in descending order (percentages optional, e.g. "cotton, elastane"). English, letters ≥1.5 mm, permanent and visible. |
| **Country of origin (clothing & footwear)** | New clothing and **footwear** (including slippers, jandals) | Jewellery, handbags, handkerchiefs, components, second-hand | "Made in …" in English, letters ≥1.5 mm. Clothing: permanent label. Footwear: marked on each item, or on a removable label or packaging if impractical. |

- Unlike AU, NZ **requires** fibre content and country of origin. A shared AU/NZ label
  template should carry care + fibre + origin.
- Online display: the rules regulate the physical label. The CIS pages do not require
  online display (care labels must be "easy to find before purchase", which online is
  arguably the listing). Showing fibre/care/origin on the product page is low-cost, so
  recommend it.

### 2.3 Product safety standards and unsafe goods notices
- Mandatory product safety standards: baby walkers, **children's nightwear**, children's
  toys, cigarette lighters, cots, ladders, bicycles, sunscreen [P]. **None apply to adult
  apparel, footwear, bags or jewellery.** NZ has no sunglasses standard, unlike AU.
- Unsafe goods notices (FTA s31): current notices include small high-powered magnets
  (**UNVERIFIED** list; the page did not list them). Check magnetic jewellery.
- Recalls: a supplier who voluntarily recalls goods must notify the Minister (MBIE Product
  Safety) within **2 working days** (s31A). NZ has **no** AU-style mandatory injury
  reporting. **UNVERIFIED** here.

## 3. Japan (medium)

### 3.1 Household Goods Quality Labeling Act (家庭用品品質表示法) [P: CAA]
- (a) **Requirements.** Designated goods must carry the legally required labelling items in
  **Japanese**: for textiles, fibre composition (%), care symbols per JIS L 0001, and for
  some items water-repellency; plus the **labeler's name and contact (Japanese address
  starting from the prefecture, and phone with area code)**. Email, URL or QR code cannot
  replace them (FAQ Q1–Q5).
- **Covered items relevant to us** (CAA list): coats, shirts, blouses, trousers, skirts,
  sweaters, dresses, underwear, sleepwear, swimwear, jackets; leather or synthetic-leather
  garments and gloves; **shoes (靴), socks**; **bags (かばん)**; **hats (帽子)**; **scarves,
  mufflers, shawls**; gloves; umbrellas; **sunglasses (サングラス)**. Not listed: Western
  belts and jewellery.
- (b) **Cross-border:** FAQ Q8 says goods sold to Japanese consumers must be labelled in
  Japanese, with a labeler that has a business base in Japan. Q9 says that when orders are
  taken on an EC site and shipped direct from abroad, labelling is still required
  "regardless of shipping route". Q10 says a Japanese consumer buying directly from an
  overseas seller for personal use (個人輸入) is **outside the Act**.
  - Our model, a foreign MoR selling DDP to a Japanese consumer, fits Q10 on its face. Q9,
    however, reads as aimed at sellers whose sales are directed at Japan, and a
    Japanese-language, JPY-priced, DDP storefront looks like that. **Grey area: get a JP
    lawyer's view before launch.** Q11: online listings are "advertising", so label items
    are not mandatory online, but displaying them is recommended.
- (c) The labeler is either manufacturer or seller (agreed between them), and must be able
  to answer consumer inquiries and inspections in Japan. Without a JP entity we cannot be
  labeler. If labelling is required, we need a JP representative or contracted labeler
  whose name goes on the label.
- (d) **Dates:** textile labelling rules revised **20 Aug 2024** to follow JIS L 0001's
  alignment with ISO 3758:2023 (new symbols 111 hand-wash 30°C and 511 no-steam iron;
  iron temperatures changed to 210/160/120°C; dry-clean P/F symbols widened). Goods
  labelled under the old rules **by 19 Aug 2025** may continue to be sold [P: CAA news
  release 20-Aug-2024]. Further amendment notice 25 Dec 2024 for textiles and miscellaneous
  goods (content not checked).
- (e) **Enforcement:** CAA plus METI/prefectures run inspections and publish annual results
  (FY2025 report 26 Jun 2026). Unlikely to reach individual B2C parcels at the border.
- (f) **Per SKU:** fibre % by part, JIS L 0001 care symbols (2024 version), item type per
  CAA list; for bags/shoes, material of outer/upper and lining or sole plus handling notes
  (**UNVERIFIED** detail); for sunglasses, lens type/UV data. Japanese text.

### 3.2 Act on Control of Household Products Containing Harmful Substances (有害物質含有家庭用品規制法)
From prior knowledge (MHLW page 404). **UNVERIFIED** this session:
- **Formaldehyde:** infant textiles (≤24 months: diapers, underwear, nightwear, gloves,
  socks, outerwear, hats, bedding) ≤16 ppm (absorbance ≤0.05). Adult **underwear,
  nightwear, gloves, socks, tabi** ≤75 ppm. Outerwear for adults is not covered.
- **Specified azo dyes** (24 aromatic amines, ≤30 µg/g each), since **1 Apr 2016**:
  textiles (diapers, underwear, nightwear, gloves, socks, middle and outer garments, hats,
  bedding, floor coverings) and **leather** (including fur) underwear, gloves, garments, hats.
- **Organotin** (TBT, TPT, not detectable): diapers, bibs, underwear, gloves, socks, plus
  sanitary goods and shoe cream.
- Flame retardants (APO, TDBPP, BDBPP) on nightwear, bedding, curtains. Dieldrin and DTTB
  moth-proofers on underwear, gloves, socks, etc.
- Obligation sits with **manufacturers and importers**, and sale of non-compliant goods is
  prohibited. A foreign seller to personal importers is probably outside direct
  enforcement. Collect azo, formaldehyde and organotin test reports anyway (they double as
  EU REACH evidence).

### 3.3 Specified Commercial Transactions Act
- Mail-order sellers, including overseas sellers to Japanese consumers, must display name,
  address, phone, price, returns terms, etc. Covered by the consumer-law researcher.

### 3.4 IP border enforcement
- A Customs Act amendment, in force **1 Oct 2022**, lets Japan Customs seize trademark- or
  design-infringing goods **sent by an overseas business to an individual** even when the
  buyer imports for personal use. **UNVERIFIED** date (prior knowledge). Impact: counterfeit
  or look-alike SKUs from VN merchants will be seized, and we carry the refund and
  chargeback. Per SKU: brand ownership or licence evidence.

## 4. South Korea (medium)

**UNVERIFIED** throughout (no primary source reached; prior knowledge to mid-2026).
- **KC framework.** The Electrical Appliances and Consumer Products Safety Control Act has
  tiers: safety certification (안전인증), safety confirmation (안전확인), supplier's
  conformity confirmation (공급자적합성확인), and safety-standard compliance (안전기준준수).
  Adult household textile products (가정용 섬유제품) and leather products (가죽제품) sit
  in the lower tiers, so **no third-party certification** is needed, but products must meet
  KC safety standards (formaldehyde, pH, azo, heavy metals for leather) and carry
  Korean-language labels: product name, fibre composition, care (KS K 0021), manufacturer or
  importer, country of manufacture. Sunglasses and fashion jewellery may have separate
  safety standards (e.g. cadmium and lead limits for metal accessories).
- **Children's products** (under 13, Special Act on Children's Products Safety): KC 안전확인
  with a third-party test is required for domestic sale.
- **Overseas direct purchase (해외직구).** Goods bought by individuals for personal use are
  exempt from KC. On 16 May 2024 the government announced it would ban direct purchases of
  80 categories (including children's products) without KC. After public backlash it
  **withdrew the plan within days (around 19–20 May 2024)**. Instead, KATS, the Korea
  Customs Service and Seoul city test products sold on AliExpress, Temu and Shein (many
  findings of phthalates and lead/cadmium in children's goods and accessories) and **block
  specific hazardous products**. Platforms signed voluntary safety agreements with the
  KFTC (May 2024). Later amendments allow ordering foreign platforms to block hazardous
  products and require large foreign e-commerce operators to appoint a domestic agent.
  Exact 2025–26 status is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **For us:** KC is not required for B2C direct-purchase parcels. We are exposed to
  post-market testing and blocking, and to KCA/KFTC orders. Recommend Korean-language
  product info on listings and lead/cadmium tests for metal jewellery.

## 5. Taiwan (medium)

**UNVERIFIED** throughout.
- **Commodity Labeling Act** and the Textile Labeling Standards (紡織品標示基準): for goods
  sold by businesses in Taiwan, the label must show, in Chinese, fibre composition, care
  (CNS 15290 symbols, aligned to ISO 3758), size, country of origin, and
  manufacturer/importer name, address and phone. Enforced by local governments at the point
  of sale. Personal-import B2C parcels are generally outside it.
- **BSMI (Commodity Inspection Act):** mandatory inspection mainly for **infant and
  children's textiles** (formaldehyde, pH, colourfastness, azo dyes per CNS 15205). Adult
  apparel and footwear are not generally on the mandatory inspection list (check the
  current BSMI list). Goods for personal use, below set quantities, are exempt from
  inspection.
- For us: low formal burden for adult goods. Children's clothing would need BSMI testing if
  imported commercially.

## 6. China (light)

**UNVERIFIED.**
- **GB 18401-2010** (basic safety for textiles: categories A infant, B skin contact, C non-skin
  contact; formaldehyde, pH, colourfastness, odour, banned azo amines). **GB 31701-2015**
  (infant and children's textiles: adds cords/drawstrings, small parts, phthalates in
  coatings). **GB 5296.4** (labelling in Chinese). Apparel is **not** under CCC.
- **Cross-border e-commerce retail import (CBEC)**, under the 2018 MOFCOM et al. notice (No.
  486) and the positive list: goods are supervised as **personal-use items**. No
  first-import licence, registration or filing, and Chinese labels are not required.
  However:
  - The overseas CBEC enterprise bears **primary responsibility for product quality and
    safety**.
  - It must appoint a **domestic agent** (境内服务商/代理人) registered with customs, which
    shares liability.
  - It must give consumers a **risk notice** that goods meet origin-country standards but
    may differ from Chinese standards.
  - Annual RMB 26,000 and per-order RMB 5,000 limits apply.
  - Customs can sample-test and recall.
- Per SKU: HS code on the positive list, GB 18401 category test (recommended).

## 7. India (light)

**UNVERIFIED.**
- **BIS Quality Control Orders.** The footwear QCOs (leather and other materials;
  rubber/polymeric, e.g. IS 6721, IS 15844, IS 17043) have been in force since mid-2023 to
  2024, staggered by manufacturer size. Imported footwear needs a BIS licence held by the
  **foreign manufacturer** (Foreign Manufacturers Certification Scheme) and the Standard
  Mark. Whether B2C courier imports for personal use are exempt is **UNVERIFIED**; customs
  has refused QCO goods without a licence. Textile fibre QCOs (polyester, viscose, etc.)
  target raw materials; several were **withdrawn in Nov 2025**. There is no general QCO on
  finished apparel, bags or fashion jewellery. Note: precious-metal jewellery hallmarking
  does not apply to fashion jewellery.
- **Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules 2011.** Pre-packaged goods for retail must
  declare importer name and address, country of origin, MRP, net quantity, and consumer-care
  contact. Since 2017–2020 amendments (rule 6(10)), **e-commerce entities must display these
  declarations, including country of origin, on the listing**.
- **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020.** They apply to e-commerce entities **not
  established in India but systematically offering goods to consumers in India**. As an
  inventory e-commerce entity we would need: a grievance officer (acknowledge within 48
  hours, resolve within 1 month); display of legal name, address, contact, returns,
  refunds, warranty, payment security, and **country of origin**; no manipulative pricing.
  A nodal officer resident in India is required only for entities incorporated in India or
  foreign companies with an Indian place of business (rule 4(1)).
- Per SKU: country of origin, MRP-style declarations, BIS licence number (footwear).

## 8. Children's clothing: where rules differ (brief)
- **AU/NZ:** children's nightwear and limited daywear fire-hazard standard (AS/NZS 1249).
- **JP:** infant (≤24 months) formaldehyde ≤16 ppm across most textiles.
- **KR:** KC 안전확인 third-party test (personal import exempt).
- **CN:** GB 31701.
- **TW:** BSMI infant textile inspection.
- **Recommendation:** exclude children's wear from the AU/NZ launch, or gate it per SKU
  behind a test report.

## 9. Data model implications (per SKU)
- `country_of_origin` (ISO 3166). Required for NZ clothing and footwear labels, IN
  listings, and customs.
- `fibre_composition` (fibre to %, by component). Required for NZ, JP, KR, TW, CN labels.
- `care_instructions` (ISO 3758 / JIS L 0001:2024 / KS K 0021 symbol codes plus text).
- `label_photos` (care, fibre, origin labels as sewn).
- `category_flags`: sunglasses (AU lens category), children's (age band), leather/fur
  (species), magnetic parts, DWR/PFAS-free declaration.
- `test_reports[]`: standard, lab, date, result (AS/NZS 1067.1; JP azo/formaldehyde;
  Pb/Cd for metal jewellery; GB 18401 category).
- `supplier_factory` (name, address) for modern slavery reporting.
- `brand_authorisation` for JP/global IP seizure risk.
- `bis_licence_no` (IN footwear).
- `jp_labeler` (only if JP labelling is required).
