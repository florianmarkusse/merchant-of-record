## 1. EU General Product Safety Regulation (EU) 2023/988

**(a) What it requires.**
- Art 5: only safe products may be placed on the market.
- Art 9 (manufacturer): internal risk analysis and technical documentation, kept 10 years.
  The product carries a type, batch or serial number and the manufacturer's
  name, postal and electronic address. Instructions and safety information
  are in a language consumers understand, as decided by the member state.
- Art 11 (importer): checks the manufacturer's duties are met and adds its
  own name and address. [R]
- Art 16: a product may only be placed on the EU market if an *economic
  operator established in the Union* is responsible for it. That operator is
  the manufacturer, the importer, an authorised representative, or a
  fulfilment service provider. It verifies the technical documentation,
  cooperates with authorities, and reports accidents. Its name and contact
  details appear on the product, packaging, parcel or an accompanying
  document. [A] fieldfisher, CMS; [R]
- Art 18: traceability of supplied and supplying operators. [R] (retention
  period: UNVERIFIED, possibly 6 years)
- Art 19, distance sales. Each offer must clearly show:
  - the manufacturer's name, trade name or trademark, postal and electronic
    address;
  - if the manufacturer is not in the EU, the Art 16 responsible person's
    name and addresses;
  - information to identify the product, including a picture and its type;
  - any warnings or safety information, in the language of the member state. [A] vimm.be; [R]
- Art 20: the manufacturer reports accidents caused by a product without
  undue delay via the **Safety Business Gateway**. Importers and distributors
  inform the manufacturer and may report directly. [R]
- Art 35-37: recall notice template and a remedy to the consumer (repair,
  replacement or refund). Notices go to all affected consumers whose data
  we hold. [R]
- **Safety Gate** (ex-RAPEX) publishes authorities' alerts. Fashion alerts
  are dominated by Cr VI in leather, azo dyes, nickel/cadmium/lead in
  jewellery, phthalates in PVC, and drawstrings on children's clothing. We
  should screen it for our merchants' SKUs.

**(b) Distance sales.** Yes. An online offer aimed at EU consumers counts as
placing on the market (Art 4 GPSR / Art 6 MSR). [R]

**(c) Who owes it. This is where the entity choice matters most.**
- **Dutch BV as MoR, selling DDP and declaring the import itself (or through
  an indirect representative):** the BV is an EU-established legal person
  that places a third-country product on the EU market. That makes it the
  **importer**, and so the Art 16 responsible person. It owes the importer
  duties: checking documentation, labelling its own name and address, and
  handling accidents and recalls. The VN merchant stays the **manufacturer**.
  If we sold under our own brand we would become the manufacturer (Art 13);
  we must not.
- **Singapore entity as MoR:** it is not "established in the Union", so it
  cannot be the importer or the responsible person in the GPSR sense. The
  courier is not a fulfilment service provider: the definition excludes
  postal and parcel delivery services [R]. The VN manufacturer must
  therefore **mandate an EU authorised representative** in writing, which
  could be an EU affiliate of ours or a third-party service. Expect friction:
  one mandate per merchant, and the representative is on the hook.
- Either way we are the distance seller, so the Art 19 listing duties sit on
  our pages.

**(d) Dates.** Applies since 13-Dec-2024. EEA incorporation is still pending
(see Norway). Commission guidance: the GPSR Q&A exists, but the EC webgate
blocked fetching, so it was not re-read.

**(e) Enforcement reality.**
- Customs and market-surveillance sweeps on small parcels have stepped up
  (2025 Commission e-commerce communication, and coordinated actions on the
  big Chinese platforms) [R/N].
- In practice, a missing EU responsible person on the parcel or listing
  gets parcels stopped and marketplaces delisting.
- For an MoR with an EU entity, the authority's easy target is us.

**(f) Per-SKU data.**
- Manufacturer legal name, postal and e-mail address.
- Product identifier: model or type, plus batch or serial.
- Photo.
- Warnings and safety information, translated.
- The manufacturer's risk analysis or technical file. Hold a copy, or get a
  contractual right to obtain it within days.
- Children's clothing: EN 14682 (cords and drawstrings), small parts and
  button pull strength.

## 2. Market Surveillance Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, Art 4

- **What it requires:** for products under the harmonisation acts listed in
  Art 4(5), an EU economic operator must hold the DoC and cooperate with
  authorities.
- **What is covered for us:** the list is mostly CE-marked goods. Apparel,
  footwear, bags and fashion jewellery are not covered. **Sunglasses are PPE
  category I** under Reg 2016/425, which is on the list. They need a CE mark,
  an EU DoC against EN ISO 12312-1 and an EU-based Art 4 operator. The same
  NL BV vs SG analysis applies: under the SG entity, the merchant needs an
  EU authorised representative. [R]
- **Per-SKU data for sunglasses:** DoC, test report, filter category, CE
  marking, and an instruction leaflet in the local language.

## 3. Textile labelling

**Textile Labelling Reg (EU) 1007/2011.**
- **What it requires:** fibre composition by weight using the harmonised
  fibre names in Annex I. The label is durable, legible and accessible.
  Products with non-textile parts of animal origin carry the phrase
  "contains non-textile parts of animal origin" (leather trims, fur, horn
  buttons). [R]
- **Distance sales:** the composition must be available to the consumer
  before purchase, so it goes on the product page. [R]
- **Language:** the label is in the official language(s) of the member state
  where the product is made available (Art 16(3)) [R]. So FR needs French,
  DE needs German, NL needs Dutch, and BE and LU need several languages. EU
  fibre names have official translations, which our catalogue can generate
  from a closed fibre enum.
- **Who owes it:** the manufacturer labels. An importer or distributor must
  not make available a product that is not labelled, so the NL BV owes the
  check. Bags and accessories with at least 80 % textile by weight count as
  textile products. [R]
- **Revision:** the Commission's proposal to revise 1007/2011 is **not
  published**. The EP legislative train (updated 25-Oct-2026) shows it as
  "blocked" by the Commission, planned "by Q2 2026" [P]. Expected content:
  a physical plus digital label, possibly folding in care, size, origin and
  sustainability information [N] (EuroCommerce, ECOS). Design the catalogue
  data model so these fields can be added.

**Care labelling.**
- Not mandatory under EU law, nor in France (GINETEX/COFREET symbols are
  voluntary but customary) [R]. UK, CH and NO: also voluntary [R].
- Collect care symbols anyway, because the TLR revision may make them
  mandatory.
- UNVERIFIED: whether any EU member state mandates care labels.

**France, other information.**
- Loi Toubon: French language for labels and the offer.
- Triman and Info-tri on textiles, footwear and packaging.
- AGEC Art 13 environmental product sheet (Décret 2022-748) [R]:
  - **Textiles and footwear:** countries of weaving, dyeing/printing and
    making (for footwear: stitching, assembly, finishing); share of recycled
    material; a warning on plastic microfibre release when synthetic content
    is above 50 %; SVHC presence.
  - **Who:** producers and importers above a phased turnover and unit
    threshold (≥ €10 M and ≥ 10,000 units from 2025) [R].
  - **Our case:** probably below threshold at first, but merchants above it
    would put the duty on us as the importer.
- New FR law 2026-602 of 08-Jul-2026 [P] (ecologie.gouv.fr):
  - Defines ultra fast fashion; the thresholds await a decree.
  - Malus of up to 50 % of the price from 01-Sep-2026.
  - Advertising ban from 01-Jan-2027.
  - Messages on sales websites about textile impact and reuse/repair (scope
    unclear).
  - Display of manufacturing locations (scope unclear).
  - [N] says a France-established representative is required for foreign
    EPR producers from 10-Jul-2026. UNVERIFIED on the official page.
- Low risk that our VN merchants hit the ultra fast fashion thresholds, but
  check once the decree is out.

**Footwear Directive 94/11/EC.**
- Label the material of three parts: upper; lining and sock; outer sole.
  Use the four categories leather, coated leather, textile and other, as
  pictograms or words in an accepted language. The material must make up
  at least 80 % of the part's surface (of the upper, for the upper). [R]
- The manufacturer or its representative labels. The retailer is
  responsible for informing consumers [R]. In distance sales, show it on
  the listing.
- **Per SKU:** three material codes.

**Wider PFAS.**
- **Universal PFAS restriction:** still at ECHA [R]. Expect it to cover all
  textiles in the future.
- **France:** Law 2025-188 and Decree 2025-1376 ban PFAS-containing consumer
  textiles, footwear and waterproofing agents from 01-Jan-2026. Stock made
  earlier can be sold until 31-Dec-2026 [N]. All textiles are covered from
  2030 [N] (SGS, Compliance & Risks).
- **Denmark:** BEK 464 bans clothing and footwear with total fluorine ≥ 50
  mg/kg from 01-Jul-2026 [N].
- **What this means for us:** require PFAS-free declarations from all
  merchants, and test any water-repellent product.

**SVHC and SCIP.**
- **Art 33:** a supplier of an article containing a Candidate List SVHC
  above 0.1 % w/w must pass that information on. A consumer who asks must
  get it within 45 days, free.
- **Art 7(2):** notification to ECHA only above 1 t/year per substance.
  Unlikely for us.
- **SCIP:** Waste Framework Directive Art 9(1)(i) requires suppliers of
  articles to notify ECHA. ECHA treats importers as obliged. Retailers that
  supply only consumers are exempt [R], but that exemption is for
  distributors who do not import, so the NL BV-as-importer probably owes
  SCIP.
- UNVERIFIED whether SCIP survives the 2025 omnibus/WFD discussions.
- **Per SKU:** an SVHC declaration (none above 0.1 %, or a list), versioned
  against the Candidate List date (updated twice a year).

**POPs (EU) 2019/1021.** Bans on PFOA, PFOS and PFHxS (water-repellent
finishes), SCCPs (PVC, leather fat-liquoring), PBDEs and others, with limits
in articles [R]. Put them on the same restricted-substances list (RSL)
declaration.

**Children's clothing (flag).**
- REACH entry 63 covers lead in articles children can put in their mouths.
- Stricter PAH limits for children's articles.
- EN 14682 drawstrings.
- UK Nightwear (Safety) Regulations 1985 on flammability.

**Merchant data model.** Use a per-SKU and per-material RSL declaration plus
test reports keyed by material and colourway:
- **Leather:** Cr VI, azo dyes.
- **Metal parts:** nickel release, Cd, Pb.
- **Plastic and rubber:** PAHs, phthalates.
- **Textiles:** azo dyes, NPE, formaldehyde, PFAS.

Store the lab, the accreditation (ISO 17025), the report date and the
substance list version.

## 5. Extended producer responsibility (EPR)

### Textiles

**EU Directive (EU) 2025/1892, amending the Waste Framework Directive, new
Art 22a.** [A] Debevoise, Cuatrecasas, Charles Russell Speechlys
- In force since 16-Oct-2025; transposition by **17-Jun-2027**.
- Mandatory textile EPR schemes in every member state by **17-Apr-2028**
  (micro-enterprises later, 2029 per [N]).
- Products in Annex IVc: clothing and accessories, footwear, household
  textiles. Whether bags and leather goods are in scope: UNVERIFIED; check
  Annex IVc.
- **Distance sellers in another member state or a third country must
  appoint an authorised representative for EPR** in each member state where
  they sell [A].
- Fees eco-modulated by durability and recyclability. Ultra fast fashion
  practices may be penalised.

**What this means for us:**
- We are the "producer" in each destination country as the distance seller.
- An NL BV is directly the producer in NL and needs representatives
  elsewhere. An SG entity needs a representative in every member state,
  including NL.

**National schemes already running.**
- **France, Refashion** (clothing, household linen, footwear). Mandatory
  since 2007. Foreign distance sellers must register with a French unique
  identifier (IDU) and, since AGEC, appoint a French authorised
  representative [R]. Units are reported every year, and the IDU appears in
  the terms of sale and on the website [R].
- **Netherlands, UPV Textiel** (Besluit UPV textiel), since 01-Jul-2023. The
  producer is whoever first makes the goods available on the Dutch market,
  foreign webshops included. Registration and reporting go through
  Stichting UPV Textiel. Foreign producers may appoint an authorised
  representative [R/N]. The upvtextiel.nl page failed to load, so fees for
  2026 are UNVERIFIED.
- **Other member states:**
  - Hungary: since 2023 [R].
  - Latvia: in place [R].
  - Spain: Ley 7/2022 with a textile EPR decree pending [R].
  - Italy: draft [R].
  - Sweden: proposal withdrawn in 2024 [R].
  - All of these UNVERIFIED for 2026 status.

**Per-SKU data:** product category code (the scheme's own categories, often
with a durability class), unit weight and fibre composition. Count units
and weight per country per year.

### Packaging

**PPWR (EU) 2025/40.** [R] Searches were exhausted before primary
confirmation, so check EUR-Lex.
- General application from **12-Aug-2026**.
- Producer definitions include distance sellers to end users in another
  member state. A producer must register in each member state's producer
  register, and **must appoint an authorised representative for EPR in each
  member state where it is not established**.
- Harmonised sorting labels from about 2028.
- Empty-space ratio of at most 50 % for e-commerce and grouped packaging,
  from 2030.
- Minimisation, recyclability grades and recycled content in phases to 2030
  and later.
- Restrictions on PFAS in food-contact packaging only (not relevant).

**National rules still apply through the PPWR EPR chapter:**
- **Germany:** VerpackG. LUCID registration plus participation in a dual
  system **before** the first parcel, shipping boxes included. Online
  marketplaces and fulfilment providers must check registration. Foreign
  sellers may use an authorised representative (since 2022) [R].
- **France:** Citeo or Léko for household packaging, plus the Triman/Info-tri
  marking.
- **Netherlands:** Afvalfonds Verpakkingen, with a tonnage threshold
  (UNVERIFIED).
- **Elsewhere:** similar in AT, ES, IT, PL and others.

**Per SKU and per parcel type:** packaging material and weight (polybag,
carton, swing tag, tissue, box), recycled content, and the PFAS-free
declaration for any food-contact item (none expected).

## 6. Ecodesign for Sustainable Products (ESPR) (EU) 2024/1781

**Destruction ban (Art 25 and Annex VII).**
- Large enterprises may not destroy unsold apparel, clothing accessories and
  footwear from **19-Jul-2026**. Medium enterprises follow from 19-Jul-2030.
  Micro and small enterprises are exempt [A] (Linklaters) [P]
  (circulareconomy.europa.eu).
- **Derogations:** Commission Delegated Regulation **(EU) 2026/296**,
  adopted 09-Feb-2026 and published in the OJ 22-Apr-2026 [P] (EUR-Lex ELI,
  europalov.no), applying from 19-Jul-2026. Ten derogations [A], including:
  - dangerous or non-compliant with mandatory chemical law;
  - IP infringement;
  - licensing restrictions;
  - physical damage or hygiene, where repair or refurbishment is not
    feasible or cost-effective;
  - defects that cannot be repaired;
  - failed donation: offered to at least three EU social-economy
    organisations.
- Records must be kept for 5 years.

**Returns.**
- ESPR's definition of "unsold consumer product" includes **products
  returned by a consumer under the right of withdrawal** [R] (ESPR Art 2
  definitions). Check the definition before relying on it: no secondary
  source in this session confirmed it.
- So a large operator holding returned items may not destroy them except
  under a derogation. Returns flow: resell, refurbish or donate, and record
  the derogation.

**Disclosure.**
- Annual disclosure of the number and weight of discarded unsold products
  per CN code, the reasons and the derogation used.
- Large companies from financial year 2025; standard format under
  Implementing Reg (EU) 2026/2 from **02-Mar-2027** [A].

**Who owes it.**
- The economic operator that discards. For returns held by us in the EU (NL
  BV), that is us, if we are large.
- Size is measured under Directive 2013/34 (as amended) [R]:
  - Large: exceeds 2 of 250 employees, €50 M turnover, €25 M balance sheet.
  - Medium: exceeds 2 of 50 employees, €15 M turnover, €7.5 M balance sheet.
- Size is measured at group level (UNVERIFIED for ESPR).
- Early on we are probably small, so exempt.
- Returns shipped back to VN are outside EU territory. UNVERIFIED how that
  interacts with "discarding" and with the EU's waste-shipment rules.

**Digital Product Passport for textiles.**
- Textiles are a priority group in the ESPR working plan (2025-2030).
- A delegated act on textile ecodesign and DPP is expected around 2027, with
  application roughly 18 months later (UNVERIFIED).
- Footwear: a study by end-2027 [N].
- The DPP will hit the manufacturer, and the importer must check it exists.
- Plan for a per-SKU data carrier (QR code) and a data set: materials,
  origin, substances of concern, durability and repairability.

## 7. Green claims

- **Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition, Dir (EU) 2024/825.**
  Applies from **27-Sep-2026**; transposition was due 27-Mar-2026 [N]. It adds
  to the UCPD blacklist:
  - generic environmental claims ("eco-friendly", "sustainable", "green")
    without recognised excellent environmental performance;
  - sustainability labels not based on certification or set by public
    authorities;
  - claims about the whole product when true of only part of it;
  - offsetting-based "climate neutral" claims;
  - durability-related practices. [R/N]
- **Who owes it:** us, as the trader making the commercial practice. The
  claims sit on our pages and invoices.
- **What we must do:** the platform must block unsubstantiated green claims
  from merchant copy. Each claim or label needs its certificate, stored per
  SKU.
- **Green Claims Directive:** the Commission announced withdrawal on
  20-Jun-2025, then said it was not formally withdrawn. Status unclear, not
  law [A] (CMS, Mondaq). Ignore for build; watch.

## 8. EU Forced Labour Regulation (EU) 2024/3015

- **What it requires:** bans placing on the market, making available
  (distance sales included) and export of products made with forced labour.
  Authorities investigate risk-based. When a breach is found: withdrawal,
  disposal (donation, recycling or destruction) at the operator's cost, and
  a block at customs.
- **Dates:** applies from **14-Dec-2027** [R]. Commission guidelines and a
  risk database are due beforehand. UNVERIFIED whether they are published.
- **Who owes it:** every economic operator, us included. There is no
  formal due-diligence duty, but evidence of due diligence matters in an
  investigation.
- **Vietnam angle:** cotton yarn and fabric imported from China, including
  Xinjiang cotton.
- **Per merchant and per SKU:** fibre origin (cotton country), main
  suppliers (fabric mill, tannery), audits and certifications.

## 9. EU Deforestation Regulation (EU) 2023/1115

- Annex I covered cattle hides and leather (HS 4101, 4104, 4107) and never
  finished leather goods: bags (4202), footwear (6403) and belts (4203) were
  not in it [R].
- **Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/2102:** adopted 13-Jul-2026, published in
  the OJ 17-Sep-2026, in force 18-Sep-2026. It **removes 4101, 4104 and 4107**
  [N] (Coolset; Linklaters headline). Leather may return at the 2030 review.
- Main application: 30-Dec-2026 for large and medium operators, 30-Jun-2027
  for micro and small [N].
- **Conclusion:** EUDR does not apply to our goods today. If a merchant ever
  ships rubber-soled footwear: natural rubber is in Annex I, but HS 6406 and
  footwear are not, to the researcher's knowledge [R]. Low risk.

## 10. Fur and exotic skins

- **CITES** (EU Wildlife Trade Reg 338/97, UK COTES, CH and NO CITES
  ordinances): python, crocodile, lizard and ostrich-type exotic leather may
  need export and import permits. Shipping such goods by express parcel to
  consumers is impractical. **Recommend excluding CITES-listed species from
  the catalogue.**
- **Cat and dog fur:** banned EU-wide (Reg 1523/2007). **Seal products:**
  banned (Reg 1007/2009) [R].
- No EU, UK or NO ban on selling fur. Fur farming bans (NL, AT, NO, and
  others) do not restrict sales [R].
- **Switzerland:** the Fur Declaration Ordinance requires declaring the
  animal species (common and Latin name), origin, and production/keeping
  method, including at online sale [R]. A Swiss import ban on fur from cruel
  methods (a counter-proposal to the fur initiative) is UNVERIFIED for 2026
  status; the BLV page returned 404.
- **Per SKU:** has_animal_fur, species and origin; exotic leather species
  and CITES status.

## 11. United Kingdom

**Product safety.**
- **GB:** General Product Safety Regulations 2005 still apply [R].
  - Duties sit on the "producer". Where the manufacturer is not
    UK-established, that is its UK representative, or failing that the
    **importer**. Distributors have lesser duties.
  - Where we are importer of record on DDP shipments, we are the importer
    for GPSR 2005. That is UNVERIFIED for a non-UK-established importer:
    the 2005 Regs do not require UK establishment as the EU GPSR does.
  - **No UK responsible person rule for general consumer goods** [R]. One
    exists only for UKCA/CE goods (sunglasses as PPE: UK PPE Regs, a UK
    importer name on the product) and cosmetics.
- **Northern Ireland:** the EU GPSR applies under the Windsor Framework
  [R], so NI consumers need an EU responsible person. GB-established
  operators may be accepted (UNVERIFIED).
- **Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025:** Royal Assent 21-Jul-2025
  [A] (Fieldfisher, Lewis Silkin, Freshfields). It gives powers to impose
  duties on online marketplaces and others. Commentators expected online
  marketplace secondary legislation to be consulted on first, possibly in
  2026 [A].
  - No 2026 instrument or consultation was found (search budget exhausted).
    UNVERIFIED.
  - We are a seller rather than a marketplace. Watch whether the duties
    reach "online sellers" and require a UK-based contact.

**UK REACH.**
- Retained Annex XVII as of end-2020: azo, Cr VI, Ni, Cd, Pb, PAHs,
  phthalates, DMFu and CMR entry 72.
- **Not** the later EU additions: no PFHxA entry 79 [R]. UK REACH
  restriction work on PFAS is pending (UNVERIFIED).
- Duties sit on GB importers, who need UK REACH registration only above 1
  t/year.

**UK textile and footwear labelling.**
- Textile Products (Labelling and Fibre Names) Regulations 2012 (GB),
  mirroring 1007/2011, in English.
- Footwear (Indication of Composition) Labelling Regulations 1995 [R].

**UK packaging EPR** [P] (GOV.UK, updated 19-Aug-2026).
- Obligated only if **established in the UK**: registered office or
  principal place of business in the UK. A foreign HQ counts as established
  if it has a **UK branch, warehouse, or UK postal or returns address**.
  Thresholds: more than 25 t of packaging and turnover of at least £1 M.
- Owning an online marketplace that supplies packaging from outside the UK
  is a listed activity. That rule targets marketplaces, not sellers.
- **Consequence:** do not set up a UK returns address without accepting UK
  packaging EPR.

**Other UK rules.**
- No UK textile EPR yet (UNVERIFIED for 2026).
- No UK destruction ban.
- CMA Green Claims Code, with direct fining powers under DMCC Act 2024
  since April 2025 [R].
- Modern Slavery Act s.54: only at £36 M turnover with a UK business
  presence.

## 12. Switzerland

- **Product safety:** Product Safety Act (PrSG, SR 930.11) and Ordinance
  (PrSV) [R].
  - Whoever places a product on the Swiss market must ensure safety, label
    contact details and traceability, and report dangerous products to SECO.
  - **No Swiss responsible person requirement** for general goods sold from
    abroad [R].
  - A PrSG revision to align with the EU GPSR is UNVERIFIED.
- **Textiles, leather and jewellery are "Gebrauchsgegenstände"** under food
  law [R]: the Federal Act on Foodstuffs and Utility Articles (LMG) and the
  Ordinance on Objects for Human Contact (HKV, SR 817.023.41).
  - Limits on azo dyes, nickel release, Cr VI in leather and similar
    substances, broadly EU-equivalent.
  - The duty of self-control falls on the person placing on the market.
- **ChemRRV (SR 814.81):** Cd and Pb in jewellery, PFAS (CH tracks EU
  restrictions with a lag; CH PFHxA adoption UNVERIFIED), and others [R].
- **Textile declaration:** no mandatory fibre-composition labelling in CH;
  if given, it must be correct (UWG) [R]. **Fur declaration mandatory**
  (§10).
- **Language:** the official language of the sale area (DE/FR/IT), for
  safety information.
- **EPR:** no mandatory textile or packaging EPR in CH. Voluntary
  industry solutions only [R].

## 13. Norway

- **EEA, but with lag.**
  - **REACH** (with Annex XVII) and the **Textile Labelling Regulation**
    apply through the EEA Agreement [R].
  - **GPSR 2023/988 is not yet incorporated into the EEA Agreement**,
    according to the Norwegian ministry's EEA note (updated 18-Jun-2026) [P]
    (europalov.no).
  - **ESPR 2024/1781 is not yet incorporated either**; it will be
    implemented via the Sustainable Products Act (bærekraftige
    produkter-loven) [P].
  - So in Norway the Product Control Act (produktkontrolloven) and the
    General Product Safety Regulations based on the old GPSD apply. There
    is no responsible person requirement yet [R].
- **Norwegian product regulation (produktforskriften):** some national
  rules (historic PFOA) [R].
- **Labelling:** textile fibre labels in Norwegian [R]. Whether Swedish or
  Danish is accepted is UNVERIFIED.
- **EPR:**
  - **Packaging:** producer responsibility under Avfallsforskriften
    chapter 7, through approved schemes such as Grønt Punkt. Thresholds and
    applicability to foreign distance sellers UNVERIFIED.
  - **Textiles:** Norwegian textile EPR has been consulted on; status
    UNVERIFIED.
- **Transparency Act (åpenhetsloven):** applies to larger enterprises that
  are resident in Norway or liable to tax there. VOEC VAT registration is
  likely not "tax liability" in that sense (UNVERIFIED).
- **Entity choice:** a NL BV is not "established" in Norway, so it is a
  foreign seller there like the SG entity.
