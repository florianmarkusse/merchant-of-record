## Cross-cutting points

1. **"Importer" duties when we sell DDP.** In most SEA regimes, label, SNI, halal-conformity
   and TISI duties sit on the *importer*. For low-value courier parcels the customs consignee
   or importer of record is usually the buyer, even under DDP: the courier pays duties on
   our account but declares the consumer as consignee. If we instead become importer of record
   (as a non-resident importer, or through a local IOR), those duties shift to us. This is a
   design decision about how the courier files the entry. **Open.**
2. **Online-seller duties fall on us directly**, whoever the importer is: ID Permendag
   31/2023, PH RA 11967, MY ETT Regs 2012, the TH DSDM Act and SG CPFTA. They mostly ask for
   disclosure (identity, address, total price, product characteristics, returns). We can meet
   them centrally in the checkout and invoice templates.
3. **Indonesia is the market most likely to be closed to us** by the US$100 FOB floor
   (Permendag 31/2023 Art. 19). It should be confirmed before launch. See ID-1.
4. **Safety testing of jewellery and children's wear** is the only hard technical item in
   this slice: SG names ASTM F2999 (adult jewellery, Pb/Cd) and ASTM F2923 (children's
   jewellery). The same tests will serve EU/US slices.
5. **Animal-derived materials**: collect leather species, wool and bone/horn per SKU now.
   It drives ID halal (from 2028 for clothing and accessories; leather goods unclear),
   CITES (VN export) and wildlife rules elsewhere.

## Singapore

### SG-1 CPSR (Controlled Goods) — not applicable
- CPSR covers "33 categories of household electrical, electronic and gas appliances and
  accessories". It is pre-market control with registration and the SAFETY Mark. [P CGSR
  booklet App. B/FAQ 5; consumerproductsafety.gov.sg CPSR page]
- None of our categories are Controlled Goods. Battery items are already out of scope.

### SG-2 Consumer Protection (Consumer Goods Safety Requirements) Regulations 2011 (CGSR) [P]
- (a) "Consumer goods shall conform to applicable safety standards." Category 1 goods (where an
  ISO/IEC/EN/ASTM standard exists) must meet that standard plus CPSO add-ons. Category 2 goods
  must meet regional or national standards. The regime is post-market only: "There is no
  requirement for pre-market testing, certification or approval from the CPSO", but
  "investigations ... will include request to access relevant test reports".
- Named in scope: "Apparel", "Costume jewellery and watches", children's products, toys.
- Listed standards relevant to us: **Adult jewellery ASTM F2999**; **children's clothing
  upper outerwear EN 14682 / ASTM F1816** (drawstrings); **children's jewellery ASTM F2923**;
  **children's sunglasses EN ISO 12312 / ISO 12312**. Adult sunglasses and footwear are not
  listed, so the general duty applies.
- Out of scope: used or second-hand products, and goods imported solely for re-export.
- (b) Applies to goods "supplied in Singapore". The booklet does not address offshore online
  sellers. In practice CPSO acts through stop-sale orders, publicising unsafe goods, and local
  importers and platforms. (c) "Supplier" includes manufacturer, importer, distributor,
  retailer and "any person who exchanges or disposes of the product for any consideration",
  so **us as seller of record**. (d) In force since 2011-04-01; booklet updated 2025-09-26.
  (e) Low enforcement on individual parcels. The risk is a stop-sale or publicised unsafe
  listing, and selling after publication is an offence (fine and/or imprisonment).
- (f) Per SKU: jewellery Pb/Cd test (ASTM F2999), and for children's lines the cord/drawstring
  conformity and ASTM F2923.
- Source: CGSR Information Booklet (updated 26 Sep 2025),
  https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/498/9dcd3fad-8da8-4d63-863b-53f130d9fa40/cgsr-info-book.pdf [P]

### SG-3/4 CPFTA and CPTDSA (UNVERIFIED, prior knowledge)
- CPFTA (Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act 2003): unfair practices include misleading
  pricing. CCCS price-transparency guidance expects the total price, including mandatory
  charges, to be shown up front (no drip pricing). For a GST-registered supplier, IRAS
  requires GST-inclusive display prices. CPFTA applies to "suppliers" dealing with SG
  consumers, and reach over overseas suppliers is asserted but rarely enforced.
- CPTDSA (Consumer Protection (Trade Descriptions and Safety Requirements) Act 1975):
  prohibits false trade descriptions, including origin and materials. Origin marking is not
  generally mandatory for apparel.
- E-commerce Marketplace Transaction Safety Ratings and Online Criminal Harms Act codes target
  designated marketplaces, not a single-seller MoR. Not applicable unless we run a
  marketplace (UNVERIFIED).

## Hong Kong (UNVERIFIED, primary pages failed to load)
- **Cap 456 Consumer Goods Safety Ordinance**: a general safety requirement on anyone who
  supplies, manufactures or imports consumer goods into HK. Warnings and safety labels must be
  in both English and Chinese. Excludes goods covered by other ordinances (food, drugs, toys
  and children's products under Cap 424, etc.). Apparel, bags and fashion jewellery are within
  "consumer goods". Enforced by Customs & Excise mainly against local traders. Offshore
  sellers are reached in practice through the importer or courier.
- **Cap 362 Trade Descriptions Ordinance**: false trade descriptions and (since the 2013
  amendments) unfair trade practices, including bait advertising and misleading omissions.
  The **Trade Descriptions (Marking) (Gold and Gold Alloy) Order** and **(Platinum) Order**
  apply if a fashion-jewellery item is described as gold or platinum (fineness marking).
  Our "gold-plated" or "gold tone" wording must be accurate.
- **Cap 424 Toys and Children's Products Safety Ordinance**: children only. Toys must meet
  listed standards, and the listed children's products (cots, walkers, etc.) are not believed
  to include clothing.
- No mandatory origin marking for apparel. HK has no duty or GST, which is customs, not this
  slice.

## Malaysia (UNVERIFIED, prior knowledge; no primary text fetched)
- **Consumer Protection (Electronic Trade Transactions) Regulations 2012** (P.U.(A) 458/2012,
  in force 2013-07-01): a person operating a business through a website or online
  marketplace must disclose its name, registration number, address and contact details, the
  main characteristics of the goods, the full price including delivery and taxes, payment
  method, terms and estimated delivery, and must let the buyer correct errors before
  confirming. The KPDN position on foreign websites selling to MY consumers is unclear.
  Treat it as applicable and meet it in checkout (cheap to do).
- **Consumer Protection Act 1999** Part III: the Minister may prescribe safety standards
  (e.g. toys, Consumer Protection (Safety Standards for Toys) Regs 2009, children only). A
  general safety duty applies to suppliers.
- **Trade Descriptions Act 2011**: false trade descriptions. Orders on price marking and
  "halal" wording exist. Whether a textile-labelling order with mandatory BM content applies
  to apparel is **UNVERIFIED**. Labelling in Bahasa Malaysia is expected for goods placed on
  the domestic market, and parcels are not policed in practice.
- No SIRIM mandatory certification believed to cover our categories.

## Thailand
- **Label-controlled goods (CPB, Consumer Protection Act B.E. 2522)**. Label-controlled goods
  are those "produced by factories in Thailand or imported into Thailand for sale" [A Tilleke].
  The label must be in Thai, and a 2023 readability rule (effective 2023-06-18) sets a minimum
  text height of 2 mm [A]. Penalties include a THB 50,000 fine and/or one month's
  imprisonment, and trading partners and platforms selling non-compliant goods can be
  penalised [A]. The OCPB held a public hearing on 2026-02-24 on the Notification specifying
  label-controlled goods [A via search summary]. **UNVERIFIED but widely reported: garments,
  footwear and bags are label-controlled**, requiring a Thai label with product type, maker
  or importer name and address, country of origin, size, usage and care, and price. A draft
  CPA amendment tightens importer labelling duties [A TÜV/Tilleke summary]. **Who owes it**:
  the importer. In DDP B2C with the buyer as consignee, enforcement against individual
  parcels is minimal. If we act as importer of record, the duty is ours.
- **TISI mandatory standards**: no adult apparel, footwear, bags or jewellery are believed to
  be on the mandatory list (UNVERIFIED). The list is dominated by electrical goods, helmets,
  toys (TIS 685) and some chemicals. 2025 reports of TISI and platform sweeps on cheap
  imports target uncertified mandatory-standard goods [N, UNVERIFIED], so they do not touch
  our scope except children's toys.
- **Direct Sales and Direct Marketing Act B.E. 2545 (2002), am. B.E. 2560 (2017)**: "if the
  online trading of goods can be completed through an online channel, the operator is
  considered a direct marketing operator and must register with the Office of the Consumer
  Protection Board" [A Tilleke Fashion Law 2024]. Registration in practice needs a Thai
  entity, and offshore sellers are not registered or pursued (UNVERIFIED). **Draft amendment**
  (OCPB consultation of 2026-11-02, comments until **2026-11-27**): it extends the definitions
  to digital platforms, adds notification for small sellers (≤ THB 1.8m/yr), extends the
  cooling-off period from 7 to **14 days**, and requires platforms to have "a local
  coordinator for offshore merchants" [A Tilleke insight dated 2026-11-06]. Watch this item.
  A 14-day withdrawal right for TH buyers affects our returns state machine.
- Foreign Business Act: offshore e-commerce into TH is not itself a "business in Thailand",
  but a local presence would be (UNVERIFIED).
- Counterfeits: the Customs IPR recordation system (TCIRs, since 2022-09) lets brand owners
  have customs impound infringing imports, exports and transits [A Tilleke].

## Indonesia
- **ID-1 Permendag 31/2023 (e-commerce)**, issued 2023-09-26, replacing Permendag 50/2020:
  - Art. 5(4): foreign merchants (pedagang luar negeri) selling via domestic PPMSE must provide
    proof of business legality from the home country, compliance with mandatory SNI and halal,
    **Bahasa Indonesia labelling** on products of foreign origin, and the ship-from origin
    [N Bisnis.com]. Proof means an SNI certificate or registration number where SNI applies,
    and a halal certificate number where halal is mandatory [N]. Art. 5(2): product
    descriptions must be in Bahasa Indonesia [N]. Art. 53: administrative sanctions [N].
  - Art. 19: PPMSE running cross-border trade must apply a **minimum price of US$100 FOB per
    unit** for finished goods sold directly by overseas merchants, except goods on a
    ministerial **positive list** (goods not produced domestically and without Indonesian IP
    or GI). Fashion is not expected to be listed [N Bisnis/Antara, UNVERIFIED list contents].
    Shopee stopped cross-border foreign-seller listings in response [N].
  - (b/c) The rule binds PPMSE (platforms). **Whether our own single-merchant checkout is a
    PPMSE "melakukan perdagangan lintas negara" is UNVERIFIED.** Under PP 80/2019 a foreign
    PPMSE actively targeting Indonesian consumers above thresholds (prior knowledge: >1,000
    transactions or parcels/year) must appoint a representative in Indonesia (UNVERIFIED).
    Assume ID is blocked for sub-US$100 apparel until Indonesian counsel says otherwise.
  - (f) Per SKU: FOB unit value, SNI applicability and certificate, halal status, Bahasa label
    and description.
- **ID-2 Halal (Law 33/2014; PP 42/2024 as amended by PP 40/2026)**:
  - BPJPH [P]: under PP 40/2026, "sandang" (clothing), "penutup kepala" (headwear) and
    "aksesoris": "masa penahapan wajib halalnya diperpanjang sampai dengan 17 Oktober 2028".
    The notice makes no size or origin split for barang gunaan, and **does not mention leather,
    footwear or bags**. Source:
    https://bpjph.halal.go.id/read/penyesuaian-penahapan-kewajiban-sertifikasi-halal-diatur-dalam-pp-40-tahun-2026-begini-rinciannya
  - PP 40/2026 was enacted 2026-10-14, and the text was public 2026-10-21 [A REACH24H; A
    Veritask]. Earlier sources treated 2026-10-17 as the deadline and said leather goods
    "including bags, belts, wallets, shoes" are in scope [N RRI quoting an official]. Leather
    goods may be grouped under accessories or a separate barang gunaan heading:
    **UNVERIFIED which date applies to them.**
  - Mechanics: foreign products are certified through an Indonesian importer or authorised
    representative [A Halal Focus]. Importers of regulated products need a Halal Product
    Conformity Report (LPPH) for customs clearance [A]. Products of non-halal origin (e.g. pig
    leather) need a "non-halal" statement instead (BPJPH Reg. 3/2026 on non-halal product info
    [A AHK]). Halal applies to *animal-derived* materials, so cotton or synthetic apparel is in
    practice certifiable but low risk. Pig leather or bristle must be labelled non-halal.
  - Small parcels: no evidence of parcel-level enforcement. Under Permendag 31/2023 the halal
    proof would be demanded by platforms.
- **ID-3 SNI**: the only SNI believed mandatory in our range is **baby clothing** (azo dyes,
    formaldehyde, extractable metals; Permenperin 7/2014, SNI 7617:2013, UNVERIFIED cite). It
    needs SPPT-SNI certification by the importer. Adult apparel, footwear (except safety
    footwear) and bags are not on the mandatory list (UNVERIFIED).
- **Bahasa Indonesia labelling**: required for goods circulating in ID. For our flow it is
    imposed directly by Permendag 31/2023 Art. 5(4) [N]. The general list of goods requiring a
    BI label (formerly Permendag 73/2015, now in the risk-based licensing standards) is
    UNVERIFIED for apparel.
- **ID-4 Import policy**: Permendag 16/2025 (effective 2025-08-30 [A DFDL]; amended by
    Permendag 18/2026 [N]): Pasal 39(4)(c) lists "Barang kiriman" as Barang Bebas Impor. A
    non-API importer may import these, and they are not for business purposes [P pasal.id].
    Restricted consumer goods (Permendag 23/2025: bags, footwear [N]) and textiles
    (Permendag 17/2025) need PI/LS for commercial import, with Pasal 41(2) exemptions for
    non-API importers [P]. Quantity limits for parcels sit in an annex not retrieved
    (UNVERIFIED). Customs parcel duty rates (e.g. 25% for bags, footwear and textiles) belong
    to the customs slice.
- **ID-5 Used clothing**: the import of used clothing is prohibited (Permendag 18/2021 as
    am. 40/2022, UNVERIFIED cite). It matters for returns or refurbished resale.

## Philippines
- **PH-1 DTI-BPS mandatory certification**: the official list's only entries near our area
  are "Helmets and their visors" and "Child Restraint Systems". It has no apparel, textiles,
  footwear, bags, jewellery, eyewear or toys [P
  https://bps.dti.gov.ph/product-certification/list-of-products-under-mandatory-certification].
  Toys and childcare articles fall under FDA (RA 10620), children only (UNVERIFIED).
- **PH-2 Internet Transactions Act RA 11967** [P lawphil]:
  - s.3 scope covers B2B and B2C where the merchant "is availing of the Philippine market"
    with minimum contacts. Under s.5 such a person "cannot evade legal liability in the
    Philippines despite lack of legal presence". s.12 gives DTI jurisdiction.
  - s.23(f): e-retailers must publish corporate name, business address and contact details on
    the homepage and **submit them to the E-Commerce Bureau with proof of identity**. s.10
    sets up an Online Business Database. s.8(c): the Bureau enforces registration of online
    merchants.
  - s.23(b)-(c): goods must match the description and have normal quality. s.25: the
    **e-retailer or online merchant is primarily liable** to the consumer. s.26-27:
    platforms are subsidiarily or solidarily liable (not relevant unless we are a platform).
  - The Act has no resident-agent requirement. The IRR (2024) and DTI orders on registration
    mechanics are UNVERIFIED.
- **PH-3 Consumer Act RA 7394** (Arts. 74-81, labelling and price tags): consumer products
  must carry the registered trade or brand name, the name and address of the manufacturer,
  importer or repacker, contents, and the country of manufacture if imported (UNVERIFIED
  article detail). It is aimed at goods sold domestically. Parcels are not policed.

## Vietnam as origin (all UNVERIFIED: thuvienphapluat blocked, no searches left)
- **VN-1 Export licensing**: Decree 69/2018 (Law on Foreign Trade Management): apparel,
  footwear, bags and fashion jewellery are not on the export-ban or export-licence lists. No
  export licence is needed. Gold and precious-metal jewellery has separate SBV/precious-metal
  rules (out of scope for fashion jewellery). Merchants export via the courier's e-commerce or
  express declaration.
- **VN-2 Labelling (Decree 43/2017, am. Decree 111/2021, in force 2022-02-15)**: for exported
  goods, label content follows the importing country's rules (Decree 43 Art. 10). Decree 111
  adds that the origin shown must be determined correctly (Art. 15 as amended: "Made in
  Vietnam" / "Sản xuất tại Việt Nam" only when VN origin is met; otherwise "assembled in",
  "finished in", etc.). False origin labels are penalised (Decree 128/2020 customs penalties;
  Decree 98/2020 for commerce).
- **VN-3 Origin (Decree 31/2018; Circular 05/2018/TT-BCT)**: Circular 05/2018 sets when goods
  are of VN origin (wholly obtained, or last substantial transformation in VN: change in
  tariff heading or ≥30% local value content, with minimal operations like cutting, packing
  or label-sewing excluded). It was adopted against transshipment, and US-bound enforcement
  is heavy. For our flow: apparel sewn in VN from Chinese fabric usually passes the CTH test
  (fabric to garment, chapter change). Garments imported finished and only relabelled do not.
  Per SKU: bill-of-materials origin and the operations done in VN. As seller of record we
  carry misdeclaration risk on our invoice, so merchant attestation is needed.
- **VN-4 CITES / wildlife leather**: VN implements CITES (Decree 06/2019 on management of
  endangered wildlife and CITES implementation, UNVERIFIED whether since replaced). Exporting
  products of CITES-listed species (crocodile, python, monitor lizard, some stingray? check)
  needs a CITES export permit from the VN CITES Management Authority, and Appendix I species
  also need a destination import permit. Recommendation: exclude exotic skins at launch, or
  require species and permit per SKU. Cow, buffalo, goat, sheep and pig leather are fine.
- **VN-5 Counterfeits**: VN Customs applies IP border controls to exports too (IP Law;
  Ministry of Finance circular on IP border control, UNVERIFIED number), and VN is a frequent
  origin for seized counterfeits. Per SKU: brand-ownership or licence evidence for any
  third-party brand.

## ASEAN-wide (UNVERIFIED)
- ACCSQ harmonisation covers electrical and electronic goods, cosmetics, medical devices,
  pharma, automotive and similar, not apparel, footwear or jewellery. There is no ASEAN
  textile-labelling or general product-safety directive. The ASEAN Agreement on E-Commerce
  (2019) has no product rules. The ASEAN DEFA negotiations are reported as substantially
  concluded, and DEFA would add e-commerce consumer protection cooperation, not product
  standards.
