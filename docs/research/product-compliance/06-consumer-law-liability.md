**Planned policy, at a glance.** Planned: a returnless refund under about US$40, a local return hub above that, and border duties not refunded.

- **Returnless refunds** are lawful everywhere, because they are more generous than the law.
- **Local hubs** are fine. In EU, UK and NO the consumer may be charged the return postage only if told so beforehand. In TW and on KR defect returns, the consumer pays nothing.
- **Not refunding duties** conflicts with the law in these cases:
  1. EU, UK and NO withdrawals: we must refund "all payments received", and the duty and tax we built into the DDP price are part of that payment.
  2. TW withdrawals.
  3. KR, almost certainly, since the refund covers the payment received. UNVERIFIED.
  4. Every market's statutory remedy for defective or misdescribed goods.

  It is lawful only for **voluntary** change-of-mind returns, and only if disclosed before the contract: US, CA, AU, NZ, SG, MY, ID, PH, HK, CH, and JP with a displayed policy.

## Part A. Consumer law per market

### A1. European Union

**Withdrawal right (CRD Art 9–16)** [K, standard text]
- **Length and start:** 14 days from the day the consumer, or a third party they name, takes physical possession. For multiple goods delivered separately, the period runs from the last item.
- **Missing information:** if we did not inform the consumer of the right, the period is extended by 12 months (Art 10).
- **Refund (Art 13):** we refund "all payments received from the consumer, including, if applicable, the costs of delivery", without undue delay and within 14 days of being informed of the withdrawal. We may hold the refund until we have the goods back, or proof they were sent, whichever comes first. We refund the same way the consumer paid, with no fees. Outbound delivery is refunded only up to the cheapest standard option we offered.
- **Return cost (Art 14(1)):** the consumer bears the direct cost of returning the goods, but only if we informed them of this beforehand. Otherwise we bear it. We may also offer to collect the goods ourselves.
- **Deductions:** we may deduct only for diminished value from handling beyond what is needed to establish the nature, characteristics and functioning of the goods (like trying on in a shop). We cannot deduct duties, taxes, payment fees or a restocking fee.
- **Duties are not deductible.** The import VAT/IOSS VAT and duty that our DDP price included are a "payment received from the consumer", so they must be refunded. Our own recovery is a separate matter:
  - IOSS VAT is corrected in the return for the period of the refund.
  - Customs duty refund under UCC Art 118 covers goods rejected as defective or not conforming to the contract. Whether a change-of-mind withdrawal qualifies is UNVERIFIED and is a question for the customs slice.
  - Below €150 there is normally no duty. The changes to that de minimis threshold are also for the customs slice.
- **Exceptions (Art 16):** none fit our catalogue normally. Sealed goods unsealed after delivery, for health or hygiene reasons, could cover earrings for pierced ears and possibly underwear and swimwear only if they are sealed. Personalised or made-to-measure goods are excepted, and that matters if merchants engrave jewellery.
- **Information duties (Art 6(1)(h), Annex I):**
  - Conditions, time limit and procedure for withdrawal.
  - The model withdrawal form (Annex I(B)). Using the model instructions (Annex I(A)) gives a safe harbour.
  - Who bears return costs (Art 6(1)(i)). If the goods cannot normally go back by post, an estimate of the cost.

**Withdrawal function or "button" (Art 11a, inserted by Directive 2023/2673, applies from 19-Jun-2026, so it applies now)** [A/N: Hogan Lovells, iubenda, Andersen; I could not fetch the EUR-Lex text]
- **Scope:** every distance contract concluded through an online interface, where a withdrawal right exists.
- **The function:** a withdrawal function that is prominent, continuously available during the withdrawal period and labelled "withdraw from contract here" or equivalent unambiguous wording. The consumer gives or confirms their name, contract identification and electronic communication channel. They then press a confirmation function labelled "confirm withdrawal" or equivalent.
- **Acknowledgement:** we acknowledge receipt on a durable medium without undue delay, including the content and the date and time of the withdrawal.
- **Pre-contract information:** must say that the function exists and where it is.
- **No obstacles:** the flow must not obstruct the withdrawal, for example with forced login or a survey first. The detailed list of banned patterns is UNVERIFIED.
- **National transposition** exists, for example Germany §356a BGB [N].
- **Build consequence:** our order pages need a no-JS, server-rendered "withdraw from contract here" → "confirm withdrawal" flow that does not require an account login. Access by order number and email link is the safe design. The flow must store an append-only withdrawal event with a timestamp and email the acknowledgement. This fits our outbox and state-machine design.

**Conformity (Sale of Goods Directive 2019/771)** [K]
- **Liability period:** minimum 2 years from delivery (Art 10). Member states may go longer: Sweden 3 years, Ireland 6 years (limitation), and in NL and FI liability follows the expected lifespan.
- **Burden of proof:** reversed for 1 year (Art 11). Some states extend this to 2 years, e.g. Portugal, and France for new goods.
- **Remedies (Art 13–16):** repair or replacement at the consumer's choice, free of charge, within a reasonable time and without significant inconvenience. Then price reduction or termination. The trader takes back replaced goods at its own expense. On termination, the refund covers the full price paid (including duties built into the DDP price).
- **No contracting out:** the rights cannot be waived (Art 21).

**Pre-contract information and pricing** [K]
- **Seller identity (CRD Art 6(1)(b)–(d)):** our identity, geographic address, phone and email, and the merchant's identity if we act on its behalf. As MoR we are the trader, so our identity is the one that matters.
- **Price (Art 6(1)(e)):** the total price including taxes, plus all delivery charges. For DDP the displayed price must be the all-in price; drip pricing of duties at checkout is a misleading omission under the UCPD.
- **Delivery date (Art 6(1)(g)):** a delivery arrangement and date. If none is agreed, delivery is due within 30 days (Art 18).
- **Order button (Art 8(2)):** must be labelled "order with obligation to pay" or equivalent. A confirmation on a durable medium must follow, at the latest at delivery (Art 8(7)).
- **Omnibus Directive 2019/2161:**
  - Price reductions must show the lowest price of the prior 30 days (PID Art 6a).
  - We must disclose whether and how reviews are verified (UCPD Annex I no 23b–c), and personalised pricing (CRD Art 6(1)(ea)).
  - Fines can reach 4% of turnover in the member state for widespread infringements.
- **Language:** member states may require their language (CRD Art 6(7)), e.g. FR, BE, PL, ES, the Baltics and SK. The practice: localise checkout, terms and emails per market.
- **Geo-blocking Regulation 2018/302:**
  - We may not block or redirect EU customers by nationality or residence without consent.
  - We must offer the same general conditions of access.
  - Payment method rules must not discriminate by card country.
  - There is **no** obligation to deliver everywhere.
  - It applies to third-country traders too.

**Does it apply to a foreign seller?** Yes, whatever our seat. Under Rome I Art 6, a trader who directs activities to the consumer's country cannot deprive them of that country's mandatory protections, even with a choice-of-law clause. Under Brussels Ia Art 18(1), the consumer can sue at home "regardless of the domicile of the other party". **So choosing an SG or an NL entity does not change which consumer law applies.** It changes enforcement reach, product liability (see B1) and GPSR responsibility. A Dutch BV can choose Dutch law for the contract, but consumers keep their home-country mandatory rules. Our terms would then carry an Art 6(2) style notice.

**Registration:** no seller registration for consumer law. (Other duties such as EPR packaging and textile registration, GPSR and VAT/IOSS are covered in other slices.)

### A2. United Kingdom [K unless marked]
- **Cancellation (CCR 2013):**
  - 14 days from delivery, extended up to 12 months if not informed (reg 36).
  - Refund [P reg 34]: all payments; delivery up to the cheapest standard option; without undue delay; within 14 days of receiving the goods back or evidence of return. Refund to the same payment method, no fee.
  - Deduction only for lost value from handling beyond what is needed. No deduction at all if we did not give the cancellation information.
  - The consumer bears the return cost only if we informed them (reg 35).
  - Duties included in the DDP price must be refunded.
- **Defects (CRA 2015):**
  - Goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described.
  - 30-day short-term right to reject, with a full refund within 14 days and no deduction.
  - Then one repair or replacement, then price reduction or final rejection. Any deduction for use is not allowed within the first 6 months.
  - Faults showing within 6 months are presumed present at delivery. Limitation is 6 years.
  - The trader bears the cost of returning rejected goods.
- **DMCC Act 2024 (Part 4 in force 6 April 2025):**
  - Under the UCPD replacement (Sch 20), drip pricing is banned: the headline price must include all unavoidable fees. For DDP that means duties and VAT in the price.
  - Fake and incentivised reviews without disclosure are banned.
  - The CMA can fine directly, up to 10% of global turnover.
  - The new subscription rules do not apply to us.
- **Language:** English. **Registration:** none for consumer law.

### A3. Switzerland [K]
- No statutory withdrawal for online purchases. A voluntary return policy is allowed, and duties can be non-refundable if disclosed.
- **Warranty (CO Art 197–210):** 2 years for consumer goods. It may be shortened or excluded in the contract, but a blanket exclusion in standard terms aimed at consumers risks being unfair under UCA Art 8. The common practice is a 2-year warranty with repair or replacement first.
- **Information (UCA Art 3(1)(s)):**
  1. Clear identity and contact details, including email.
  2. The technical steps to conclude the contract.
  3. A way to correct input errors.
  4. A confirmation without delay.
- **Prices (Price Disclosure Ordinance):** in CHF, including VAT and public fees. Foreign-currency display is allowed alongside.
- **Language:** none mandated for online sales. DE, FR or IT is customary.

### A4. Norway [K]
- **Withdrawal:** Angrerettloven 2014 (the CRD equivalent) gives 14 days, with the same refund and cost rules as the EU.
- **Defects:** under Forbrukerkjøpsloven the consumer must notify within 2 years, or 5 years for goods meant to last much longer. The Forbrukertilsynet and the tribunal have applied 5 years to some quality footwear and outerwear. Notification must come within a reasonable time after discovery.
- **Language:** Norwegian or Scandinavian strongly expected; the Marketing Control Act enforces it.

### A5. Australia
- **Coverage:** the ACL applies to overseas online businesses that sell directly to Australians [P ACCC].
- **No cooling-off for online purchases.** Cooling-off exists for unsolicited consumer agreements only, and voluntary change-of-mind returns are allowed. A store may refuse change-of-mind returns if it says so. Signs such as "no refunds" that imply guarantee rights are excluded are misleading (ACL s 18, 29(1)(m)). Our policy must state that statutory guarantee rights are unaffected. [K]
- **Consumer guarantees (ACL s 51–59):** acceptable quality, fit for purpose and matching the description, for a reasonable period. They cannot be excluded (s 64).
  - Major failure: the consumer chooses a refund or a replacement.
  - Minor failure: we may choose to repair.
  - Where the return involves significant cost, the supplier collects at its own expense (s 263(3)).
  - A refund for failure must include the full price, i.e. duties paid as part of the price. [K]
- **Prices:** component pricing (ACL s 48) requires the single total price to be displayed prominently, so the DDP all-in price is fine. Treasury's planned drip-pricing and unfair trading reforms are UNVERIFIED.

### A6. New Zealand [K]
- **Consumer Guarantees Act 1993:** acceptable quality and so on, for a reasonable period. Contracting out is not allowed for consumers.
  - Failure of a substantial character: the consumer may reject and get a refund.
  - Otherwise: remedy within a reasonable time.
  - The supplier must collect at its own cost if return involves significant cost.
- **No cooling-off for online sales.** Uninvited direct sales get 5 working days, which does not apply to a consumer-initiated web purchase.
- **Fair Trading Act 1986:** prohibits misleading conduct and unfair contract terms. The Trader disclosure regulations 2013 require online sellers to disclose that they are in trade.
- **Overseas sellers:** the Commerce Commission treats them as covered when they supply to NZ consumers.

### A7. United States [K]
- **No federal right of withdrawal.**
- **FTC Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule (16 CFR 435):**
  - We must have a reasonable basis for any stated shipping time; with no time stated, the order must ship within 30 days.
  - If it is delayed, we must send a delay-option notice; the buyer may cancel.
  - When we cancel, the refund is due within 7 working days, or within one billing cycle for card payments.
- **State return-policy disclosure laws:**
  - CA Civ Code §1723: a retailer that does not give at least a 7-day full refund, exchange or credit must post its policy. Otherwise the buyer may return within 30 days.
  - NY GBL §218-a: the policy must be posted conspicuously, or the consumer may return within 30 days for a refund or credit.
  - FL Stat §501.142: if no policy is posted, the buyer may return within 20 days.
  - Whether these reach out-of-state online sellers is UNVERIFIED. The safe practice is to post the policy before purchase.
- **Price display:**
  - CA SB 478 (Civ 1770(a)(29), from 01-Jul-2024): advertised prices must include all mandatory fees, apart from government taxes and shipping.
  - The FTC fees rule (16 CFR 464, from May 2025) covers live events and lodging only.
  - The FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR 465, from Oct 2024) bans fake reviews.
- **Warranties:**
  - UCC 2-314 implied merchantability applies to us as a merchant seller. A conspicuous disclaimer is possible in some states and banned in others (e.g. MA, MD, and others for consumer goods).
  - Magnuson-Moss applies only if we give a written warranty. Then we must use the full or limited designation, the item must cost over US$15 for disclosure, and we may not disclaim implied warranties. Advice: give no written warranty in the US.
- **Chargebacks are a de facto return right.** Issuers allow disputes for not received, not as described or defective goods, and for credit not processed. Card networks give roughly 120 days. A clear, posted and generous returns flow is the main way to keep chargebacks down.

### A8. Canada [K]
- **No general cooling-off for distance sales.**
- **Ontario (CPA 2002 s 37–40, O. Reg 17/05, internet agreements):**
  - Before the contract: prescribed disclosure (supplier name and address, description, itemised price, delivery date, cancellation, return and refund policy), and the express opportunity to accept, decline or correct.
  - A copy within 15 days.
  - The consumer may cancel within 7 days of receiving the copy if the disclosure was missing, or if goods are not delivered within 30 days of the specified date.
  - Refund within 15 days of cancellation. Card chargeback rights apply.
  - The Consumer Protection Act, 2023 is to replace the 2002 Act; its in-force date is UNVERIFIED.
- **Quebec (CPA s 54.1–54.16):**
  - Similar rules: cancel within 7 days if the disclosure or copy was missing, or 30 days after the delivery date passes; refund within 15 days.
  - The card issuer must reverse the charge on request (s 54.14).
  - The legal warranty of reasonable durability (s 37–38) cannot be excluded.
  - The Charter of the French Language, as amended by Bill 96 (packaging and labelling rules from 01-Jun-2025), requires the website, contracts of adhesion and invoices to be in French, or in French first.
- **Coverage:** the provincial Acts apply where the consumer is in the province.

### A9. Singapore [K]
- **No cooling-off for online sales.** The CPFTA cancellation right covers direct sales, at a door or away from business premises, and timeshares.
- **Defects:** the Lemon Law (CPFTA Part 3A) applies to goods that fail to conform within 6 months (presumed present at delivery). Repair or replacement, then reduction or rescission. SOGA implied terms of satisfactory quality apply.
- **Prices:** the CCCS Price Transparency Guidelines (2024) and CPFTA Second Schedule bar drip pricing, so show the all-in price.
- **Overseas sellers:** whether the CPFTA covers them is UNVERIFIED.
- **Registration:** none.

### A10. Malaysia [K]
- **Consumer Protection (Electronic Trade Transactions) Regulations 2012:**
  1. Name, registration number, address and contact.
  2. Description, total price including shipping and taxes, payment methods and terms.
  3. Estimated delivery.
  4. A way to correct errors before the order.
  5. Acknowledgement of receipt without delay.
- **No cooling-off.**
- **Defects:** CPA 1999 Part V implied guarantees of acceptable quality and so on, which cannot be excluded.
- **Disputes:** the Tribunal for Consumer Claims hears claims up to RM50k.
- **Coverage of foreign online suppliers:** UNVERIFIED.

### A11. Thailand — NEEDS LOCAL COUNSEL
- **Direct Sales and Direct Marketing Act B.E. 2545, amended B.E. 2560 (2017):**
  - Direct marketing is selling to consumers by remote communication, including online.
  - The consumer may cancel within 7 days of receiving the goods, with no reason needed. The operator refunds within 15 days of receiving the cancellation notice. [K, UNVERIFIED details]
  - Direct marketing operators must register with the OCPB. The 2017 amendment adjusted exemptions. Whether a foreign seller with no Thai presence must or can register is **UNVERIFIED**, and it is a gating question for Thailand.
- **Defects:** Civil and Commercial Code sale warranty. The Unsafe Goods Liability Act B.E. 2551 makes sellers and importers liable for unsafe goods (B1).

### A12. Indonesia [K, UNVERIFIED details]
- **Consumer Protection Law UU 8/1999:** the right to correct information, and to compensation or replacement if goods are not as agreed (Art 4, 7). Compensation within 7 days of the transaction (Art 19(3)). Information and labels in Bahasa Indonesia (Art 8 and Law 24/2009).
- **PP 80/2019 on e-commerce:**
  - Foreign actors that actively target Indonesian consumers above thresholds (Permendag 50/2020: about 1,000 transactions or packages a year) are deemed physically present and must appoint a local representative.
  - Merchants must provide accurate information and honour returns or exchanges for non-conforming goods.
- **No change-of-mind right.**

### A13. Philippines [P lawphil RA 11967 unless marked]
- **Internet Transactions Act 2023 (RA 11967):**
  - It covers online merchants "availing of the Philippine market" with minimum contacts (Sec 3). Such a merchant "cannot evade legal liability in the Philippines despite lack of legal presence" (Sec 5).
  - The E-Commerce Bureau enforces registration of online merchants (Sec 8(c)) and keeps an Online Business Database (Sec 10). The implementing rules' registration mechanics for foreign merchants are UNVERIFIED.
  - E-retailers must publish their name, physical address and contact details on the homepage (Sec 23(f)(1)).
  - The merchant is "primarily liable" to the consumer (Sec 25).
  - Defect remedies are repair, replacement or refund, with goods recovered "without any cost to the online consumer" (Sec 20).
  - Orders that are paid or in transit generally cannot be cancelled (Sec 19), so there is **no change-of-mind right**.
- **Consumer Act RA 7394:** implied warranties and labelling in English or Filipino. [K]

### A14. Hong Kong [K]
- **No cooling-off** for online goods. The Sale of Goods Ordinance gives implied merchantable quality.
- **Trade Descriptions Ordinance:** covers misleading omissions and bait or drip practices.
- **Language:** English or Chinese.

### A15. Japan [K]
- **Specified Commercial Transactions Act, mail-order sales (通信販売):**
  - **Display duty (Art 11):** seller name, address, phone, representative, price including tax and shipping, payment, delivery time and return policy (返品特約). This is the "特定商取引法に基づく表記" page.
  - **Final confirmation screen (Art 12-6, from 2022):** must show quantity, price, payment, delivery, cancellation and return terms. Misleading screens let the consumer rescind.
  - **Default return right (Art 15-3):** if the return policy is **not** clearly displayed, including on the final screen, the buyer may return within **8 days of receipt**, at the **buyer's** cost. If we display a policy, that policy governs, and it may exclude returns or exclude duties.
- **Defects:** Civil Code contract non-conformity, with notice within 1 year of discovery. The Consumer Contract Act voids clauses that fully exempt the seller.
- **Foreign sellers:** the SCTA display duties apply to foreign sellers targeting JP. Show the information in Japanese.

### A16. South Korea [K]
- **Act on Consumer Protection in Electronic Commerce, Art 17:**
  - Withdrawal within 7 days of receiving the goods.
  - If the goods differ from the description or the contract, within 3 months of receipt or 30 days of when the consumer knew, whichever ends first.
  - Exceptions: goods lost or damaged by the consumer, value much reduced by use, or, where we have disclosed it, packaging opened.
- **Return cost (Art 18):** the consumer pays the return cost for change of mind. The **seller** pays it for misdescribed or defective goods. No penalty or damages may be charged for withdrawal.
- **Refund:** within **3 business days** of receiving the goods back. Late refunds carry statutory delay interest.
- **Foreign businesses:** large foreign operators must appoint a domestic agent (thresholds UNVERIFIED; we are likely below them). KFTC enforcement against foreign sellers is growing.
- **Language:** Korean.

### A17. Taiwan [P law.moj.gov.tw, CPA]
- **Art 19:** return or written rescission within 7 days "upon receipt of goods", with no reason needed. The consumer is **not responsible for any expenses or costs**. The period starts only once rescission information is provided, and is lost 4 months after expiry. The Executive Yuan's list of "reasonable exceptions" covers personalised goods and similar. Contrary agreements are void.
- **Art 19-2:** we retrieve the goods within 15 days of the notice, then refund within 15 days of retrieval or receipt.
- **So in Taiwan we pay the return and cannot keep duties.** The safe design is returnless or a local hub, with the label at our cost. Taiwan is a market where the returnless threshold could be set higher.

## Part B. Product liability and the MoR role

### B1. Are we strictly liable as seller of record?

**EU: new Product Liability Directive 2024/2853** (transposition by 09-Dec-2026; applies to products placed on the market after that date) [P streamlex Art 8; A Bird & Bird, Reed Smith]
- **Liable parties (Art 8(1)):** the manufacturer, and, for manufacturers outside the Union, "the importer", "the authorised representative" and, if neither is in the EU, the "fulfilment service provider". Liability is strict, for defects causing death, injury, damage to property or data loss.
- **Distributor fallback (Art 8(3)):** if no EU-established operator can be identified, **each distributor** is liable, unless it names an EU operator or its own supplier within **one month** of the injured person's request.
- **Online platforms:** Art 8(4) extends the fallback to platforms that present themselves as the seller (DSA Art 6(3) conditions).
- **NL BV as importer of record:** if a Dutch BV imports the goods DDP and is established in the EU, it is very likely the **"importer"**, a first-tier strictly liable party for every EU sale. The PLD's "placing on the market" by an EU-established operator brings in the same chain as GPSR and the Market Surveillance Regulation's "responsible economic operator".
- **SG company:** it is not an "importer" established in the Union. It is a distributor, liable only under the one-month identification fallback, or as a fulfilment service provider if it warehouses or dispatches. In practice:
  - the claimant sues the VN manufacturer, which is hard;
  - a GPSR responsible person would exist anyway, and could be an EU AR service, who then becomes the target;
  - and because we are the seller, the consumer still has contract claims against us under SGD and national law.
- **Global-e model:** in its Shopify terms, Global-e makes the **shopper** the importer of record (§1.3) and itself the exporter of record. A consumer importing for own use is not "placing on the market", so the importer tier may be empty. The effect of that choice on PLD and GPSR exposure needs EU counsel. UNVERIFIED.
- **Practical rule, whatever the entity:** keep, per SKU, the manufacturer's legal name and address, the merchant (supplier), and the batch and lot, so that we can answer an identification request within one month.

**UK: Consumer Protection Act 1987 s 2** [K]
- The producer is liable, and so is the person who imported the product into the UK in the course of business.
- A supplier, which includes us as seller, is liable unless it names its supplier or the producer or importer within a reasonable time of a request.
- If we import DDP, we are probably the "importer into the UK" and strictly liable.

**Australia: ACL Part 3-5 (s 138–141) and s 7** [K]
- Where the manufacturer has no place of business in Australia, the importer is deemed the manufacturer (s 7(1)(e)).
- If the injured person cannot identify the manufacturer, they may ask a supplier. A supplier that does not name the manufacturer or its own supplier within 30 days is treated as the manufacturer (s 147).
- An MoR selling DDP from VN is very likely the importer, or at least the supplier, so expect to be deemed manufacturer.
- Consumer guarantee claims (s 271) also run against the "manufacturer", including a deemed one.

**Switzerland (PrHG Art 2)** and **Norway (Produktansvarsloven)**: the importer is treated as the producer, and the supplier is liable if it fails to name the producer. [K]

**Thailand: Unsafe Goods Liability Act B.E. 2551.** The operators liable include the importer and **the seller who cannot identify the manufacturer or importer**. Liability is joint and several, with punitive damages possible. [K]

**United States** [K]
- **Our liability:** a seller in the chain of distribution is strictly liable in tort (Restatement (Second) §402A; Restatement (Third) Products Liability §1). An MoR that takes title and sells is a "seller" without doubt. The Amazon cases turned on whether Amazon was a seller:
  - *Bolger v. Amazon* (Cal. Ct. App. 2020) and *Loomis v. Amazon* (Cal. Ct. App. 2021): Amazon was liable.
  - *Erie Ins. v. Amazon* (4th Cir. 2019, MD law) and *Amazon v. McMillan* (Tex. 2021): Amazon was not a seller, because it did not hold title.
  - We would be on the liable side of that line.
- **Innocent-seller statutes** (e.g. Tex CPRC §82.003, 735 ILCS 5/2-621, and others) usually **exclude** cases where the manufacturer is not subject to US jurisdiction or is insolvent. A VN manufacturer therefore leaves us exposed.
- **Defence costs and verdicts** drive US risk. Fashion goods are low hazard: the main risks are jewellery (lead, cadmium, allergy), footwear slips and falls, strap or buckle failures, and flammability.
- **California Prop 65:** private-enforcer suits target online sellers of fashion jewellery, bags and footwear for lead, cadmium, DEHP and BPA. The 2018 rules put the warning duty mainly on manufacturers and importers, but retailers are liable when they import. Businesses with fewer than 10 employees are exempt. [K]

**Asia (JP, KR, TW, PH, ID, MY, SG, HK):** product liability statutes vary.
- JP PL Act and KR PL Act: importers count as manufacturers.
- TW CPA Art 7–9: importers count as manufacturers.
- PH: RA 7394 Art 97.
- ID: UU 8/1999 Art 19 and 21: the importer is liable as producer.
- Elsewhere: negligence or contract.

The common pattern is that **the importer into the country is treated as manufacturer**. Where we act as DDP importer of record, assume strict liability. [K]

**Conclusion:** whichever entity we choose, an MoR selling DDP should budget for being **the strictly liable party** in most markets. This is true wherever it is the importer, and wherever it cannot quickly name the manufacturer.

### B2. How existing MoRs split compliance and liability

**Global-e (Shopify Managed Markets Service Terms, v3, updated 26-Jan-2026)** [P, s3.global-e.com]
- **Role:** Global-e acts as merchant of record and exporter of record (§1.1); the **shopper** is importer of record (§1.3).
- **Merchant warranties (§5.1):**
  - compliance with "applicable safety, environmental, and ethical standards";
  - "proper product labeling, tags, manuals, brochures or certifications", and the origin of products or materials;
  - sales "will not infringe any rights of any individual or entity worldwide";
  - the merchant is "solely and fully liable for any errors" in product data.
- **§5.2:** the merchant is "solely responsible for all Product warranties", IP, counterfeit and safety claims, and product-related consumer claims.
- **§6.2:** the catalogue must be "updated, accurate and complete". Global-e relies on it, "at Merchant's sole liability", for HS codes, duty and tax thresholds and sellability. Weights must be accurate (§2.7).
- **Insurance (§5.4):** the merchant must carry "general/public liability insurance (including product liability)", valid in all destinations. No minimum amount is stated.
- **Merchant indemnity (§10.3):** covers breach, violation of law or third-party rights, transaction disputes, fraud, and "death, bodily harm or tangible property damage caused by a Product". Recalls are not express.
- **Global-e's own liability:** capped at the fees of the prior 12 months, with no consequential damages, and the service is "as is" (§10.1–10.2).
- **Refunds (§3.9):** the merchant authorises them. The refunded amount, *including shipping, taxes and duties*, is a Permitted Deduction from the merchant's payout, and Global-e keeps its fees. Global-e's model therefore refunds duties to shoppers and charges them back to the merchant.
- **Other money terms:** chargebacks are the merchant's cost (§3.10). Duty estimates are guaranteed when Global-e's contracted carrier is used (§3.2).
- **Terms of sale:** Global-e's own Terms of Sale govern the shopper contract (§3.5). The merchant handles cancellations and warranty claims (§3.8, §5.2).
- **Law and venue:** Delaware law and courts, with CISG excluded (§11.2).
- **KYC:** Global-e runs AML and KYC checks and may terminate immediately if they fail [P].
- **Restricted and prohibited items:** Shopify Managed Markets restricts all batteries and battery-powered items, items needing US export licences, alcohol, medical devices, weapons and similar. Global-e separates items "restricted" per country from items "blocked" everywhere; for headless stores the restriction blocks checkout only. [P help.shopify.com, docs.global-e.com]

**ESW (eShopWorld):** its merchant agreements are not public. Reportedly a similar model: the retailer warrants product compliance and data and indemnifies; ESW is MoR. UNVERIFIED.

**Paddle (digital goods only):** Paddle acts as reseller and MoR. The seller warrants that the product is lawful and non-infringing and indemnifies Paddle. It is not relevant to physical product liability. [K]

**Takeaway:** the industry pattern is that the MoR owns tax, payment, checkout and the consumer contract, while the merchant warrants the product, owns product-data accuracy, indemnifies (including product liability), carries insurance, and bears refunds and chargebacks through payout deductions. The MoR caps its own liability at its fees.

### B3. Recommended contract model, insurance and own checks

**Why we cannot copy Global-e as is**
- Our merchants are small Vietnamese companies. An indemnity from them is only as good as their balance sheet and our ability to enforce it.
- Vietnam is a party to the New York Convention, so an **arbitration award (SIAC or VIAC seat)** is far more enforceable in VN than a foreign court judgment. [K]
- If a Dutch BV is the EU importer, we cannot contract out of first-tier PLD liability towards consumers. We can only recover it from the merchant.

**Merchant agreement clauses (recommended)**
1. **Product compliance warranty** for every destination we enable. This covers:
   - chemicals: REACH Annex XVII nickel release, Cr(VI) in leather, azo dyes, lead and cadmium in jewellery; CA Prop 65; US state PFAS and textile laws;
   - labelling: fibre content, care labels, country of origin, and the US Textile Act and Care Labeling Rule;
   - GPSR information, no children's products, no counterfeits, no CITES-listed materials (exotic skins), and fur bans.
2. **Product data warranty:** HS code proposal, origin, materials and composition, weight, images, manufacturer name and address, and batch or lot. The merchant is liable for errors, including customs penalties and re-assessments.
3. **Traceability and identification:** the merchant must answer within 5 business days so we can meet the 1-month PLD fallback and the 30-day ACL s 147 deadline.
4. **Indemnity:** product liability (injury and property damage), recalls and corrective actions, IP, regulatory fines tied to the product, and customs penalties from bad data. It should be uncapped, or capped at no less than the insurance limit, and **survive termination** for the limitation period (EU PLD: 10 years long-stop, 25 for latent personal injury).
5. **Insurance:** the merchant carries product and public liability with worldwide territory, *including the US and Canada* where we enable them, at least US$1m per occurrence. We are an **additional insured** (vendor's endorsement), with a certificate on onboarding and renewal and 30 days' notice of cancellation.
6. **Financial security:**
   - a rolling reserve or holdback from payouts, e.g. 5–10% for 90–180 days, sized to returns, chargebacks and a claims buffer;
   - set-off rights;
   - possibly a parent or director guarantee for higher-risk merchants.
7. **Returns and refunds cost allocation:** statutory refunds, including duties, return labels where we must pay and returnless refunds, are deducted from payouts, following the Global-e §3.9 model.
8. **Recall cooperation:** we decide on market withdrawal; the merchant pays.
9. **Our right to delist and block SKUs** per destination, with no liability.
10. **Our liability cap** at 12 months of fees, but carved out for our own statutory duties and our errors in tax computation.
11. **Governing law and disputes:** Singapore law, with SIAC arbitration seated in Singapore. VIAC is an alternative.

**Insurance for us (the MoR)**
- We should carry our own **product liability policy**. A seller's or distributor's PL policy with **worldwide jurisdiction including US and CA** is the expensive part, and a contingent or vendor's liability layer covers the case where merchant cover fails. Also needed: errors and omissions or professional indemnity for tax and customs errors, and crime and cyber cover.
- **Indicative cost:** UNVERIFIED, from general market knowledge rather than a quote. For a small, low-hazard apparel and accessories book with US exposure, a US$1–2m limit typically costs a few thousand US$ a year. The minimum premium is often US$1.5–5k, rated on turnover at roughly 0.1–0.5%. Excluding the US and Canada cuts it materially, and jewellery or leather raises it slightly.
- Get quotes from a Lloyd's or Singapore broker before launch. Many small VN merchants will not have worldwide PL cover, so a **group or master policy** that we buy and recharge per order may be the practical way to get coverage.

**What we must do ourselves (we cannot rely on merchant warranties)**
- **The consumer contract and its duties.** This covers:
  - pre-contract information: our identity and address, the all-in DDP price, delivery estimates, return and withdrawal information and the model form;
  - the order button wording and confirmation emails;
  - the EU withdrawal function (Art 11a);
  - the 30-day prior-price rule on discounts and review-verification disclosures;
  - language localisation;
  - the JP SCTA page and final screen;
  - KR, TW and TH (if confirmed) withdrawal flows;
  - refund deadlines (KR 3 business days is the tightest).
- **Honouring statutory remedies for defects** in every market, whatever the merchant does, and then recovering from the merchant.
- **Merchant KYB, sanctions and AML.**
- **Category gating:**
  - an adult-only catalogue, with no children's products (which bring CPSIA, toy and child-safety rules);
  - batteries and electronics excluded (smartwatches);
  - cosmetics and fragrance excluded;
  - CITES and exotic skins, real fur and ivory excluded.
- **Risk-based verification of high-risk lines:** lab reports, from an accredited lab and per supplier or batch, for jewellery (nickel, lead, cadmium) and leather (Cr(VI), azo dyes). Spot checks and test buys.
- **IP and counterfeit screening** of brands and images.
- **Recall monitoring:** EU Safety Gate, UK OPSS, CPSC, ACCC and so on, with SKU blocking.
- **GPSR (EU):** online listings must show manufacturer, responsible person, product identifier and warnings; this is another slice.
- **Record keeping** of the rule and version of the returns policy shown to each buyer at checkout. This fits our "store the rule applied" principle.

## Part C. What our returns policy must at least allow

| Market | Minimum the policy must allow | Duties non-refundable lawful? |
|---|---|---|
| EU, NO | 14-day no-reason withdrawal from delivery. The consumer pays return postage only if told beforehand. Full refund including outbound standard shipping and **all duty and tax in the price**, within 14 days. Online withdrawal button. Plus 2-year conformity (5 years in NO for durable goods) at our cost. | **No** |
| UK | Same as the EU for cancellation. CRA 30-day reject and repairs at our cost. | **No** |
| TW | 7-day no-reason withdrawal; **we pay return costs**; we collect within 15 days, then refund within 15 days. | **No** |
| KR | 7-day withdrawal (consumer pays return postage). 3 months for misdescription or defect (we pay). Refund within 3 business days. | Almost certainly no (UNVERIFIED) |
| TH | Likely 7-day cancellation and 15-day refund; registration unclear | UNVERIFIED, assume no |
| JP | Any policy, if displayed (including on the final screen). Otherwise an 8-day return at the buyer's cost. | Yes, if displayed |
| US, CA, AU, NZ, SG, MY, ID, PH, HK, CH | Change of mind is voluntary; the policy must be posted before purchase (CA, NY, FL; Ontario and Quebec disclosures). Statutory guarantee or defect remedies at our cost, with a full refund of the price paid. Say that statutory rights are unaffected (AU, NZ). | Yes, for change of mind, if disclosed; **no** for defect remedies |

**Design implications for the codebase** (for a later plan, not built now):
- The return and refund policy is an **effective-dated rule per destination**, like the tax rules. The version shown at checkout is stored with the order.
- The withdrawal deadline is computed from the **delivery timestamp**, which the carrier event provides, so we must capture delivery events.
- The refund amount is computed per reason:
  - withdrawal includes duties in EU, UK, NO, TW and KR;
  - a defect refund always includes everything;
  - a voluntary return follows our policy.
- Refunding duties creates a booked loss or a recoverable receivable (a duty drawback claim, or a deduction from the merchant), never a dropped remainder.
